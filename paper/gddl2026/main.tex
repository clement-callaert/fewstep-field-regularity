\begin{abstract}
Few-step sampling of a continuous-time generative model requires a
probability path, a solver, and a budget of field evaluations (NFE).
Chen et al.\ propose minimizing averaged squared Jacobian regularity
$A_2$ as a path-selection criterion. We ask whether the order that this
scalar induces between two interpolants is reliable at fixed NFE.
Already for independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$, the exact integrals are
$5\pi/8-1$ (linear) and $\pi^2/16$ (trigonometric VP), while Heun at
NFE~$8$ reverses Gaussian $\wtwo$. The linear factor is the rational
$6797469/3559400$; a rational enclosure in $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$
gives $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$. Three regimes then separate. As a
pairwise comparator, linear versus VP invert in $5$ of $12$
geometry$\times$solver cells ($4$ of $12$ at every NFE in
$\{8,16,32\}$). As an in-family objective, trigonometric VP versus the
scalar log-covariance schedule (Chen Ex.~3.3 with $M=\lambda_{\max}$)
invert in 9 of 36 blocks and $4$ of $12$ cells. The unconstrained
per-mode minimizer attains the smallest $\cR$ and the smallest $\wtwo$
in $36$ of $36$ blocks, but is not a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant
for $d\geq 2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Few-step sampling commits to a path, a solver, and a small NFE budget
before any error is observed. Lipschitz constants enter classical
one-step bounds~\citep{hairer1993solving}.
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing averaged squared drift
Lipschitzness (their Definition~3.2, denoted $A_2$) as a
\emph{selection} criterion. They prove no bound relating $A_2$ to a
discretization error. We test whether the pairwise order that $A_2$
induces between two admissible paths is reliable at equal NFE.

We isolate that ranking question on commuting Gaussian interpolants,
where the field is affine, Gaussian $\wtwo$ is closed-form, and the
regularity integrand is known exactly. The leading result is a
deterministic scalar Heun example
(Section~\ref{sec:scalar}). A modal identity then records the signed
information that $\cR$ discards (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).
Section~\ref{sec:third} splits the ranking question into three regimes:
pairwise linear versus VP, in-family VP versus the shared Chen Ex.~3.3
scalar schedule, and the unconstrained per-mode minimizer, which is not
a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$.

Contemporaneous work studies Lipschitz Euler
rates~\citep{stephanovitch2026lipschitz}, sharpness-aware
grids~\citep{gupta2026sharpen}, least-action
paths~\citep{du2026lagrangian}, and instance-aware
discretizations~\citep{yuan2026instance}. Those results control other
objects. \citet{tao2026strain} give the general strain/vorticity form
of the phenomenon; Section~\ref{sec:related} records the precise
relation.

\section{Gaussian flows and averaged regularity}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be
independent, and let $(\alpha,\sigma)$ satisfy
$\alpha(0)=\sigma(1)=1$ and $\alpha(1)=\sigma(0)=0$. The interpolant
$X_t=\alpha X_0+\sigma X_1$~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow}
is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and covariance
$Q=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$. The marginal field is affine,
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A=C_t Q^{-1},\qquad
  c=\dot m-Am,
\end{equation}
with $C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$. If
$\Sigma_0=I$, then $Q$, $Q'$, $A$, and $\Sigma_1$ share the eigenbasis
of $\Sigma_1$, and mode $i$ obeys $x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with
$q_i=\alpha^2+\lambda_i\sigma^2$ and $a_i=q_i'/(2q_i)$. The covariance
drift formula is independent of the mean path $c(t)$. We compare the
linear path $(1-t,t)$ and trigonometric VP
$(\cos(\pi t/2),\sin(\pi t/2))$. Following
\citet[Definition~3.2]{lipschitzguided2025},
$\cR[b]=\int_0^1\|A(t)\|_2^2\,dt$. For state-independent $A$ this
coincides with their path expectation. By definition $\cR$ ignores
$c(t)$. Error is Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint
laws~\citep{gelbrich1990formula}.
This is neither pathwise trajectory error nor local truncation error.
Equal NFE uses $S=B/s$ steps with $s=1,2,4$ for Euler, Heun, and RK4.

Any admissible variance path from $1$ to $\lambda>0$ obeys
$\int_0^1 a\,dt=\tfrac12\ln\lambda$. Hence
$\cR=\int_0^1 a^2\,dt=(\int a)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)\ge(\tfrac12\ln\lambda)^2$,
with equality if and only if $a$ is constant. The minimizer is
$q(t)=\lambda^t$, $a\equiv\tfrac12\ln\lambda$, which is Chen et al.\
Example~3.3. In several dimensions, the per-mode schedule
$q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ yields
$\cR=(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|)^2$. It is not in general a shared
$(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant.

\section{A one-dimensional Gaussian ranking counterexample}
\label{sec:scalar}

\begin{assumption}
Endpoints $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$ are independent. Schedules are linear
and trigonometric VP. The solver is explicit Heun with NFE $8$ (four
steps of size $1/4$).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:scalar}
Under the assumption,
$\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1>\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\pi^2/16$, while the
Heun factors satisfy $r_{\mathrm{lin}}=6797469/3559400$ and
$0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$. Hence
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear variance is $q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=1-2t+5t^2$. Completing the
square gives the equivalent identities
$q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=5\bigl[(t-1/5)^2+(2/5)^2\bigr]
=5(t-1/5)^2+4/5$. The substitution $u=t-1/5=(2/5)\tan\theta$ converts
$\int a_{\mathrm{lin}}^2$ into $(5/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. Limits
$\arctan(-1/2)$ and $\arctan 2$ together with
$\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2)=\pi/2$ yield $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$.
The VP integral evaluates to $\pi^2/16$ by a Weierstrass identity.
The four linear Heun factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$,
$497/370$, and $97/74$, all positive. Exact VP grid values are
$a(0)=a(1)=0$, $a(1/2)=3\pi/10$, and
$a(1/4)=(9+15\sqrt{2})\pi/82$,
$a(3/4)=(-9+15\sqrt{2})\pi/82$. Positivity of $a(3/4)$ is
$15^2\cdot 2-9^2=369>0$. The four-step product lies in
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$ with nonnegative coefficients, so
substituting $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ is valid and yields
$r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ by an integer comparison in the anonymous
supplement. Interval arithmetic is a cross-check only.
\end{proof}

\section{Sign change and transported defects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

For any independent linear coupling,
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(0)=-1<0<a_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)=+1$. The VP drift vanishes
at the endpoints and does not change sign on $(0,1)$. Averaging
$a^2$ therefore erases a qualitative distinction that every
fixed-stage Runge--Kutta method samples.

Let $e_{n}=\sqrt{q(t_{n+1})/q(t_n)}$ and let $r_n$ be the numerical
factor. The telescoping identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n} r_{n}-\prod_{n} e_{n}
  =\sum_{n}(r_{n}-e_{n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{j}\Bigr)
\end{equation}
is an accounting tool, not the content of the inversion. The content
is the leading exact-minus-method coefficient:
$\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for Euler and $(-a''/12+a^3/6)h^3$ for the
implemented Heun factor. Those expansions involve different
derivatives, signs, and stage locations.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The $\cR$-minimizer and the inversion region}{The R-minimizer and the inversion region}}
\label{sec:third}

Source $N(0,I)$. Four centered targets: anisotropic Gaussians with
condition number $4$ in dimensions $2$ and $8$ (eigenvalues a
geometric sequence from $1/2$ to $2$); low-rank covariances
$FF^\top+0.05I$ of rank $2$ (seed $271828$). Because $\cR$ uses the
spectral norm, the two anisotropic dimensions share the same $\cR$
pair $(0.972296,0.192270)$, so the grid contains three distinct
linear-versus-VP $\cR$ comparisons. Each geometry is run under Euler,
Heun, and RK4 at NFE $\{8,16,32\}$. Headline $\cR$ is the continuous
integral of $\max_i a_i(t)^2$.

The per-mode log-covariance path $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ has the smallest
$\cR$ on every geometry and the smallest Gaussian $\wtwo$ in all $36$
blocks (Table~\ref{tab:threepath} reports Heun at NFE~$8$); it is not a
shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$. Linear versus VP
invert in $5$ of $12$ geometry$\times$solver cells; $4$ of those $12$
invert at every tested NFE. Trigonometric VP versus the shared Chen
Ex.~3.3 scalar schedule ($M=\lambda_{\max}$) invert in 9 of 36
blocks and $4$ of $12$ cells: anisotropic $d=2$, Euler at NFE $16$ and
$32$; anisotropic $d=8$, Euler at NFE $8$, $16$, and $32$; low-rank
$d=2$, Euler at NFE $8$ and Heun at NFE $8$, $16$, and $32$; low-rank
$d=8$, none. The fourteen inverted linear-versus-VP solver-budget rows
are a hierarchical listing of those cells, not a population rate.

\section{Related work and limitations}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and interpolants supply the Gaussian
family~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Lipschitz-guided design proposes the selection criterion
tested~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}. Solver-specific schedules and exact
linear parts are documented in diffusion
practice~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm,lu2022dpmsolver,zhang2023deis}.
\citet{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} prove sharp Lipschitz regularity of
canonical flow-matching fields and Euler-type $\wtwo$ rates; that is a
bound, not a pairwise ranking of two interpolants at equal NFE.

\citet{tao2026strain} decompose $\nabla v=S+\Omega$ and control Euler
error by the logarithmic norm $\mu_2=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, which is
signed, whereas $\cR$ integrates $\|A\|_2^2$. In one dimension
$\mu_2(a)=a$ and $\|A\|_2=|a|$. Their Theorem~5 states that McCann
displacement interpolation has vanishing material derivative, hence
Euler is order $2$ in the Eulerian analysis; Remark~7 records the
stronger Lagrangian exactness observed on that interpolant. Neither
statement applies to the independent-coupling linear or VP path
studied here. The present paper supplies a rationally certified
Gaussian instance of ranking failure for the unsigned average
$\|A\|_2^2$. A counterexample to $\mu_2$ itself is outside our
scope.

The systems are commuting affine Gaussian interpolants, three
fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods, and small equal-NFE budgets. No
learned neural field is evaluated. Chen et al.\ prove no
discretization-error bound and do not claim a universal pairwise
ranking at fixed NFE. As an in-family objective, VP versus the shared
Ex.~3.3 scalar schedule still invert in 9 of 36 blocks; $\cR$ is
usable as an objective only when its minimizer is admissible. The
Gaussian family is a
probe in which $\cR$ and $\wtwo$ are closed form; in a learned field
the linear part is not exact, so exponential integrators that solve an
affine scalar mode exactly are not a drop-in substitute. Cell counts
are hierarchical descriptive summaries, not population rates.
