\begin{abstract}
Few-step sampling in flow matching and stochastic interpolants requires
choosing an interpolation schedule before endpoint error is observed.
Averaged squared Lipschitzness of the drift, measured by the
time-integrated squared $2$-norm of its spatial Jacobian, is a plausible
schedule-design criterion, but it is not a solver-specific
discretization-error functional. Chen, Vanden-Eijnden, and Xu propose
minimizing this quantity, denoted $A_2$; they do not claim or prove that
$A_2$ universally ranks equal-NFE solver error. We study that narrower
surrogate question. For independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$, the exact
regularity integrals are $5\pi/8-1$ and $\pi^2/16$, so regularity prefers
trigonometric VP, whereas explicit Heun, a two-stage Runge--Kutta method,
with a budget of eight function evaluations (NFE~$8$), prefers the linear
path in Gaussian Wasserstein-2 distance. The inversion is certified by
an exact rational Heun product and a nonnegative element of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. A complementary construction shows that, for
every step count $N$ and every admissible endpoint log-scale, the unique
integrated-regularity minimizer can have strictly larger $N$-step Euler
endpoint error than a higher-regularity competitor aligned with that
solver grid. A specified finite Gaussian enumeration of four candidate
paths on $36$ tested blocks contains both agreement and pairwise
disagreement. These results limit a universal surrogate interpretation;
they do not evaluate learned velocity fields or estimate a population
failure frequency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Few-step sampling commits to a path, a solver, and a small NFE budget
before any error is observed. Lipschitz constants enter classical
one-step bounds~\citep{hairer1993solving}.
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing $A_2$, the
averaged squared Lipschitzness of the drift (their Definition~3.2), as
a schedule-design objective in a specified admissible family. They do
not claim or prove that $A_2$ universally ranks equal-NFE solver error.
We study that narrower surrogate question on a flow-matching marginal ODE.

The object is an integrated unsigned Jacobian energy. For a fixed
finite-step solver, endpoint error depends on stagewise field values,
temporal derivatives, quadrature structure, and signed cancellations
that this energy does not determine. For independent $N(0,1)$ and
$N(0,4)$, exact regularity prefers trigonometric VP while Heun at
NFE~$8$ prefers the linear path in Gaussian $\wtwo$
(Section~\ref{sec:scalar}). A solver-grid-aware construction then shows
that even the global $\cR$-minimizer need not minimize fixed-grid Euler
error (Section~\ref{sec:gridaware}); the competitor is built from the
solver resolution. A finite Gaussian enumeration of four specified
candidate paths on $36$ tested blocks contains both agreement and
pairwise disagreement (Section~\ref{sec:third}). No learned field is
used.

Venue relevance is through distribution learning, Wasserstein endpoint
error, and the geometry of Gaussian covariance interpolation, not
through a new geometric architecture or a practical OT algorithm.
\citet{tao2026strain} give a broader dynamical error analysis, including
material derivatives, strain, vorticity, logarithmic norms, and learned
models. The present contribution is narrower but exact and auditable: a
certified scalar Gaussian ranking inversion, a closed-form $\wtwo$
comparison, a grid-aware impossibility construction, and a transparent
finite enumeration. Contemporaneous Lipschitz Euler
rates~\citep{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} bound a different object.

\section{Gaussian interpolants and averaged regularity}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be
independent. A shared schedule is a scalar pair $(\alpha,\sigma)$. We
distinguish three regularity classes.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Class~S: $\alpha,\sigma\in C^1([0,1])$ with
  $\alpha(0)=\sigma(1)=1$, $\alpha(1)=\sigma(0)=0$, and
  $(\alpha(t),\sigma(t))\neq(0,0)$ on $[0,1]$. Linear and
  trigonometric VP belong to Class~S.
\item Class~Q: a $C^1$ path of SPD covariances $Q(t)$ with continuous
  $A=\tfrac12 Q'Q^{-1}$. The per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ is
  Class~Q.
\item Class~R: $\alpha^2,\sigma^2\in C^1([0,1])$, $\alpha^2\ge 0$,
  $\sigma^2\ge 0$, $\alpha^2+\sigma^2>0$, and
  $\alpha(0)^2=1$, $\sigma(0)^2=0$, $\alpha(1)^2=0$, $\sigma(1)^2=1$.
  Square roots need not be $C^1$ at the endpoints. Chen Ex.~3.3,
  $\sigma(t)^2=(M^t-1)/(M-1)$ and $\alpha(t)^2=(M-M^t)/(M-1)$ for
  $M>0$, $M\neq 1$, is Class~R and Class~Q, not Class~S. The $M=1$
  limit is $\sigma(t)^2=t$, $\alpha(t)^2=1-t$.
\end{itemize}
The interpolant $X_t=\alpha X_0+\sigma X_1$~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow}
is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and covariance
$Q=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$. The marginal field is affine,
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A=C_t Q^{-1},\qquad
  c=\dot m-Am.
\end{equation}
The formula
$C_t=\tfrac12(\alpha^2)'\,\Sigma_0+\tfrac12(\sigma^2)'\,\Sigma_1$
extends continuously to $[0,1]$ for Class~R; equivalently
$C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$ on $(0,1)$
where the coefficients are differentiable and nonzero. This is a flow-matching
marginal ODE, not a score-based probability-flow ODE in the sense
of~\citet{song2021score}. If $\Sigma_0=I$, then $Q$, $Q'$, $A$, and
$\Sigma_1$ share the eigenbasis of $\Sigma_1$, and mode $i$ obeys
$x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with $q_i=\alpha^2+\lambda_i\sigma^2$ and
$a_i=q_i'/(2q_i)$. The covariance drift formula is independent of the
mean path $c(t)$. We compare the linear path $(1-t,t)$ and trigonometric
VP $(\cos(\pi t/2),\sin(\pi t/2))$. Following
\citet[Definition~3.2]{lipschitzguided2025},
$\cR[b]=\int_0^1\|A(t)\|_2^2\,dt$. For state-independent $A$ this
coincides with their path expectation. By definition $\cR$ ignores
$c(t)$. Error is Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint
laws~\citep{gelbrich1990formula}.
This is neither pathwise trajectory error nor local truncation error.
Equal NFE uses $S=B/s$ steps with $s=1,2,4$ for Euler, Heun, and RK4.

Any regular variance path from $1$ to $\lambda>0$ obeys
$\int_0^1 a\,dt=\tfrac12\ln\lambda$. Hence
$\cR=\int_0^1 a^2\,dt=(\int a)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)\ge(\tfrac12\ln\lambda)^2$,
with equality if and only if $a$ is constant. The one-dimensional
minimizer is $q(t)=\lambda^t$, $a\equiv\tfrac12\ln\lambda$, which is
Chen et al.\ Example~3.3. Linear and trigonometric VP are $C^1$ shared
interpolants. In several dimensions, the per-mode
schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ yields
$\cR=(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|)^2$. When $\Sigma_1$ has at least two
distinct eigenvalues, that path is not a Class~S shared interpolant.

\section{A one-dimensional Gaussian ranking counterexample}
\label{sec:scalar}

\begin{assumption}
Endpoints $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$ are independent. Schedules are linear
and trigonometric VP. The solver is explicit Heun with NFE $8$ (four
steps of size $1/4$).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:scalar}
Under the assumption,
$\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1>\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\pi^2/16$, while the
Heun factors satisfy $r_{\mathrm{lin}}=6797469/3559400$ and
$0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$. Hence
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear variance is $q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=1-2t+5t^2$. Completing the
square gives the equivalent identities
$q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=5\bigl[(t-1/5)^2+(2/5)^2\bigr]
=5(t-1/5)^2+4/5$. The substitution $u=t-1/5=(2/5)\tan\theta$ converts
$\int a_{\mathrm{lin}}^2$ into $(5/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. Limits
$\arctan(-1/2)$ and $\arctan 2$ together with
$\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2)=\pi/2$ yield $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$.
The VP integral evaluates to $\pi^2/16$ by a Weierstrass identity.
The comparison $5\pi/8-1>\pi^2/16$ rearranges to $(\pi-2)(\pi-8)<0$,
which holds because $2<\pi<8$.
The four linear Heun factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$,
$497/370$, and $97/74$, all positive. Exact VP grid values are
$a(0)=a(1)=0$, $a(1/2)=3\pi/10$, and
$a(1/4)=(9+15\sqrt{2})\pi/82$,
$a(3/4)=(-9+15\sqrt{2})\pi/82$. Positivity of $a(3/4)$ is
$15^2\cdot 2-9^2=369>0$. The four-step product lies in
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$ with nonnegative coefficients, so
substituting $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ is valid and yields
$r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ by an integer comparison in
Appendix~\ref{app:vpheun}. Interval arithmetic is a cross-check only.
\end{proof}

\section{Sign change and transported defects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

For any independent linear coupling,
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(0)=-1<0<a_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)=+1$. The VP drift vanishes
at the endpoints and does not change sign on $(0,1)$. Averaging
$a^2$ does not retain that sign change. Fixed-stage
Runge--Kutta methods use signed stage evaluations that are not
determined by $\cR$. The sign difference is consistent with the
observed inversion, but the analysis does not isolate it as a causal
contribution.

Let $e_{n}=\sqrt{q(t_{n+1})/q(t_n)}$ and let $r_n$ be the numerical
factor. The telescoping identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n} r_{n}-\prod_{n} e_{n}
  =\sum_{n}(r_{n}-e_{n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{j}\Bigr)
\end{equation}
is an accounting tool, not the content of the inversion. The content
is the leading exact-minus-method coefficient:
$\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for Euler and $(-a''/12+a^3/6)h^3$ for the
implemented Heun factor. Those expansions involve different
derivatives, signs, and stage locations.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Four-path enumeration and the inversion region}{Four-path enumeration and the inversion region}}
\label{sec:third}

Source $N(0,I)$. Four centered targets: anisotropic Gaussians with
condition number $4$ in dimensions $2$ and $8$ (eigenvalues a
geometric sequence from $1/2$ to $2$); factor-plus-noise covariances
$\Sigma=FF^\top+0.05I$ with $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times 2}$ i.i.d.\
standard normal (seed $271828$). When $d=2$ the factor rank equals the
ambient dimension. Because $\cR$ uses the
spectral norm, the two anisotropic dimensions share the same $\cR$
pair $(0.972296,0.192270)$, so the grid contains three distinct
linear-versus-VP $\cR$ comparisons. Each geometry is run under Euler,
Heun, and RK4 at NFE $\{8,16,32\}$. Headline $\cR$ is the continuous
integral of $\max_i a_i(t)^2$. Ranking inversions were identified in
Phase~3; the counts below are a confirmatory Phase~4 reproduction on
configurations frozen before the rerun.

The per-mode log-covariance path $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ is one of four
specified candidate paths. Among those four paths on the stated $36$
tested blocks it has the smallest $\cR$ on every geometry and the
smallest Gaussian $\wtwo$ in all $36$ tested blocks
(Table~\ref{tab:threepath} reports Heun at NFE~$8$).
This finite enumeration does not imply that a global $\cR$-minimizer
minimizes fixed-NFE error. When $\Sigma_1$ has at least two distinct
eigenvalues, that path is not a $C^1$ shared interpolant. Linear versus VP
invert in $5$ of $12$ geometry$\times$solver cells; $4$ of those $12$
invert at every tested NFE. As a post-hoc pairwise comparison within the
shared-schedule class, trigonometric VP versus Chen Ex.~3.3 with
$M=\lambda_{\max}$ invert in $9$ of $36$ tested
blocks and $4$ of $12$ cells.

\section{A grid-aware Class~S Euler construction}
\label{sec:gridaware}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:gridaware}
Fix $L>0$ and an integer $N\ge 1$. Let $h=1/N$ and let forward Euler use
left endpoints $t_n=nh$ on $[0,1]$. Set
$\varepsilon_N=N(e^{L/N}-1)-L>0$, $a_0(t)\equiv L$, and
$a_{1,N}(t)=L+\varepsilon_N\cos(2\pi Nt)$. For $x'=a(t)x$, $x(0)=1$, and
the Class~S embeddings
$q_k=\exp(2\int_0^t a_k)$,
$\alpha_k=\sqrt{q_k}\cos(\pi t/2)$,
$\sigma_k=\sqrt{q_k/e^{2L}}\sin(\pi t/2)$:
both exact endpoints equal $e^{L}$; $a_0$ uniquely minimizes $\int a^2$
among scalar fields with integral $L$; Euler is exact for $a_{1,N}$ and
strictly inexact for $a_0$. Endpoint error is $|r-e^{L}|$, equivalently
Gaussian $\wtwo$ between $N(0,r^2)$ and $N(0,e^{2L})$.
\end{theorem}

The competitor is constructed as a function of the solver grid. Phase
offsets and non-grid frequencies destroy exact Euler aliasing. This is a
solver-grid-dependent impossibility result, not a model of natural
schedules, and not the mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and interpolants supply the Gaussian
family~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Lipschitz-guided design proposes the selection criterion
tested~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}. Solver-specific schedules and exact
linear parts are documented in diffusion
practice~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm,lu2022dpmsolver,zhang2023deis}.
\citet{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} prove sharp Lipschitz regularity of
canonical flow-matching fields and Euler-type $\wtwo$ rates; that is a
bound, not a pairwise ranking of two interpolants at equal NFE.

\citet{tao2026strain} decompose $\nabla v=S+\Omega$ and control Euler
error by the logarithmic norm $\mu_2=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, which is
signed, whereas $\cR$ integrates $\|A\|_2^2$. In one dimension
$\mu_2(a)=a$ and $\|A\|_2=|a|$. Their Theorem~5 states that McCann
displacement interpolation has vanishing material derivative, hence
Euler is order $2$ in the Eulerian analysis; Remark~7 records the
stronger Lagrangian exactness observed on that interpolant. Neither
statement applies to the independent-coupling linear or VP path
studied here. The present paper supplies a rationally certified
Gaussian instance of ranking failure for the unsigned average
$\|A\|_2^2$. A counterexample to $\mu_2$ itself is outside our
scope. \citet{benton2024error} bound $\wtwo$ of a learned flow; that is
an error bound, not a pairwise ranking. Consistency
models~\citep{song2023consistency} distill few-step maps.
Complementary solver-aware methods use temporal information rather than
an integrated spatial-Jacobian energy.
\citet{gupta2026sharpen} calibrate Euler grids from trajectory
acceleration on pretrained flow-matching models. The present
contribution is neither a sampler nor a training regularizer: it gives
a certified ranking non-implication and a solver-grid-aware
impossibility result for the stated classes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The systems are commuting affine Gaussian interpolants, three
fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods, and small equal-NFE budgets. No
learned neural field is evaluated. Chen et al.\ do not claim or prove a
discretization-error bound, nor that $A_2$ universally ranks equal-NFE
solver error. Among four specified candidate paths, the
per-mode path wins $36$ of $36$ tested blocks; that does not
imply that a global $\cR$-minimizer minimizes fixed-NFE error.
Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware} is grid-aligned by construction. Cell
counts are hierarchical descriptive summaries, not population rates.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An integrated unsigned Jacobian-energy functional does not contain
enough solver-specific temporal information to universally determine
few-step endpoint error. Averaged squared Jacobian regularity can
misrank equal-NFE Gaussian $\wtwo$ already for two $C^1$ interpolants of
independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$. A grid-aware construction shows that
even the global $\cR$-minimizer need not minimize fixed-grid Euler
error. A specified finite enumeration contains both agreement ($36$ of
$36$ tested blocks among four candidate paths) and pairwise
disagreement.

\section{Rational VP Heun certificate}
\label{app:vpheun}

This appendix records the integer comparison used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}. It is part of this PDF, not a separate
supplement file.

\looseness=-1
The four-step VP Heun product is the following element $P(\pi,\sqrt{2})$
of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$, obtained by expanding the four Heun
factors with $h=1/4$ and reducing $\sqrt{2}^2=2$:
{\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P(\pi,\sqrt{2})
&= 1+\tfrac{3}{40}\pi+\tfrac{15}{164}\pi\sqrt{2}
+\tfrac{78579}{10758400}\pi^2+\tfrac{45}{5248}\pi^2\sqrt{2}
+\tfrac{11421}{17213440}\pi^3
+\tfrac{4671}{17213440}\pi^3\sqrt{2}\\
&\quad+\tfrac{5103}{275415040}\pi^4
+\tfrac{567}{55083008}\pi^4\sqrt{2}
+\tfrac{243}{2203320320}\pi^5
+\tfrac{729}{2203320320}\pi^5\sqrt{2}
+\tfrac{729}{176265625600}\pi^6.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
}
Each of the twelve coefficients is a ratio of nonnegative integers,
hence nonnegative, and the constant term is $1$. Substituting
$\pi<355/113$ and $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ is therefore valid and yields
\[
P\Bigl(\frac{355}{113},\frac{99}{70}\Bigr)
=\frac{192113353671412470139303}{102753427879939708813312}
<\frac{187}{100},
\]
equivalently
\[
19211335367141247013930300<19214891013548725548089344.
\]
Hence $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ and $W_2^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
The bound $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ is $99^2-2\cdot 70^2=1$;
$\pi<355/113$ follows from Machin's formula with Leibniz remainders
(seven Taylor terms for $\arctan(1/5)$, four for $\arctan(1/239)$).
An independent \texttt{mpmath}~$1.3.0$ interval enclosure is a
\mbox{cross-check}, not the proof.
