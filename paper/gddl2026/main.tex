\begin{abstract}
Choosing a probability path for few-step generative sampling is often guided
by averaged regularity of the velocity field: a path with smaller averaged
squared Jacobian norm is expected to discretize better at a fixed number of
function evaluations (NFE). We test this assumption where every quantity is
exact: commuting Gaussian probability flows, whose numerical endpoint laws
and Gaussian 2-Wasserstein ($\wtwo$) errors are closed-form. The
averaged-regularity ordering of linear versus variance-preserving paths
fails to determine the fixed-NFE $\wtwo$ ordering: fourteen ranking
inversions reproduce across Euler, Heun, and RK4, survive an 80-digit
precision audit, and recur in a pre-registered non-centered family. An exact modal decomposition explains the
mismatch through solver-stage sampling and signed one-step defects
transported to the endpoint, and an explicit scalar construction proves the
non-implication for fixed-grid Euler. This is a controlled limitation
study: it does not refute regularity-guided schedule design, and no learned
model is evaluated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Few-step sampling of continuous-time generative models commits to a
probability path, a solver, and a small NFE budget before any error is
observed. A natural criterion, made explicit by Lipschitz-guided schedule
design~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}, ranks candidate paths by the averaged
squared Jacobian (Lipschitz) norm of the velocity field. Averaged
regularity controls worst-case error
\emph{bounds}~\citep{hairer1993solving}, but bounding an error is weaker
than determining which of two paths errs less at an equal small budget. We
isolate that question where the marginal flow is affine, the fixed-step
endpoint law is exactly computable, and the error is the closed-form
Gaussian $\wtwo$~\citep{gelbrich1990formula}: no sampling, training, or
estimation noise intervenes, and every reported digit is auditable.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A controlled exact-$\wtwo$ benchmark on commuting Gaussian flows in
  which the averaged-regularity ordering fails to determine the fixed-NFE
  $\wtwo$ ordering in 14 of 36 pre-specified comparisons, verified against
  an 80-digit reference and reproduced (11 of 18 comparisons) in a
  pre-registered non-centered family frozen before execution.
  \item An exact eigenmode analysis showing the endpoint factor error is a
  transported sum of signed, solver-stage-dependent local defects---%
  information an unsigned time average of Jacobian norms cannot retain.
  \item An explicit smooth scalar construction proving that, for every
  $L>0$ and grid size $N$, strictly larger averaged squared Jacobian is
  compatible with zero fixed-grid Euler endpoint error.
\end{itemize}

Solver dependence of good schedules is not itself
novel~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm}; the contribution is the exact,
controlled non-implication and its modal explanation. Averaged regularity
may well remain useful for schedule \emph{design}; our result bounds what
it can certify about fixed-budget \emph{rankings} in the tested systems.

\section{Gaussian probability flows and averaged regularity}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be
independent and let a scalar schedule $(\alpha(t),\sigma(t))$, with
$\alpha(0)=\sigma(1)=1$ and $\alpha(1)=\sigma(0)=0$, define the interpolant
$X_t=\alpha X_0+\sigma X_1$~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow}.
The marginal law is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and
covariance $Q(t)=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$, and the marginal
velocity field is affine,
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A(t)=C_t\,Q(t)^{-1},\qquad
  c(t)=\dot m_t-A(t)\,m_t,
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with $C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$; centered
with $\Sigma_0=I$ this is $A(t)=\tfrac12 Q'Q^{-1}$, $c\equiv0$. We compare
the \emph{linear} path $(1-t,\,t)$ and the \emph{variance-preserving} (VP)
path $(\cos\tfrac{\pi t}{2},\,\sin\tfrac{\pi t}{2})$~\citep{song2021score}.
Following \citet{lipschitzguided2025}, the averaged-regularity baseline is
$\mathcal{R}[b]=\int_0^1\|A(t)\|_2^2\,dt$ (exact spectral norms, fixed
trapezoidal grid); note $\mathcal{R}$ ignores $c(t)$.

A fixed-step solver applied to \eqref{eq:affine} induces an affine endpoint
map, recovered exactly from $d{+}1$ probe trajectories; pushing the source
moments through it and applying the Gaussian $\wtwo$
formula~\citep{gelbrich1990formula} gives the exact endpoint error. With
$\Sigma_0=I$ and $\Sigma_1=U\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)U^{\top}$ all matrices
commute: each mode obeys $x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with $a_i=q_i'/(2q_i)$,
$q_i=\alpha^2+\sigma^2\lambda_i$, and in the centered case
$\wtwo^2=\sum_i(|r_i|-\sqrt{\lambda_i})^2$, where $r_i$ is the product of
the numerical one-step factors of mode $i$.

\section{Why the ranking need not agree: transported signed defects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The exact one-step transition of mode $i$ over $[t_n,t_{n+1}]$ is
$e_{i,n}=\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$, while a Runge--Kutta step
multiplies by a factor $r_{i,n}$ built from stage samples of $a_i$ (one for
Euler, two for Heun, four for RK4). For smooth $a$, the leading local
defect (exact minus method) is $\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for Euler and
$(-\tfrac1{12}a''+\tfrac16a^3)h^3$ for Heun---different derivatives, signs,
and stage locations. The endpoint factor error obeys the exact telescoping
identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n<S}r_{i,n}-\prod_{n<S}e_{i,n}
  =\sum_{n<S}(r_{i,n}-e_{i,n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{i,j}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{i,j}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
with no smallness or positivity assumption. This identifies what the
scalar $\mathcal{R}$ discards: which times the stages sample, the
derivatives of $A$, the \emph{signs} of local defects, their cancellation,
and their transport to the endpoint. An unsigned average of $\|A\|^2$
retains none of these, so it need not determine the fixed-NFE ordering.

\section{Controlled benchmark and results}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Design.} Source $N(0,I)$; anisotropic (condition number 4) and
seeded low-rank ($FF^{\top}{+}0.05I$, rank 2) targets; $d\in\{2,8\}$;
linear and VP paths; Euler, Heun, RK4; NFE $\in\{8,16,32\}$ with
equal-NFE accounting (steps $=$ NFE$/$stages): 72 float64 CPU
configurations in 36 two-path blocks. A \emph{ranking inversion} is a
block where the $\mathcal{R}$ and $\wtwo$ orderings strictly disagree.
Grid, definitions, and tolerances were fixed before execution; post-hoc
diagnostics are labeled as such.

\textbf{Fourteen audited inversions.} All 72 configurations reproduce a
registered earlier run within $10^{-12}$, and 14 of 36 blocks are
inversions (Figure~\ref{fig:inversions};
\artifact{phase4\_gaussian\_reproduction\_2026-07-24-v1:results}). The
strongest is the low-rank target, $d=8$, Euler, NFE 8
(Table~\ref{tab:strongest}): the baseline prefers linear while its $\wtwo$
is $78\%$ larger (margin $0.3543761036$). All 72 float64 $\wtwo$ values
agree with an 80-digit \texttt{mpmath} modal reference to at most
$9.71\times10^{-10}$; the smallest surviving inversion margin,
$1.1189\times10^{-5}$, exceeds this by a factor above 11{,}500
(\artifact{phase4\_precision\_2026-07-24-v1:table}).

\textbf{Solver--path interaction.} In the low-rank grid, VP is preferred
under Euler and linear under Heun and RK4, at both dimensions and every
primary budget (Figure~\ref{fig:interaction}); the pattern persists at NFE
64 and 128 and under $\pm10\%$ target perturbations
(\artifact{phase4\_robustness\_2026-07-24-v1:table}). Solver-dependent
schedule preference is established~\citep{sabour2024align}; the point is
that a solver-independent scalar cannot represent it. A solver-specific
leading proxy from Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} agrees with 29 of 36
observed preferences versus 22 of 36 for the baseline, but this is post-hoc
and in-sample and carries no predictive claim
(\artifact{phase4\_diagnostics\_2026-07-24-v1:table}).

\textbf{Pre-registered external validation.} To leave the original
design space we froze, before execution, one non-centered family: source
$N(\mu_0,I)$, $\mu_{0,i}=0.75(-1)^i$; target $N(\mu_1,D)$,
$\mu_{1,i}=1+0.25i$, $D$ geometric with condition number 6---so
$c(t)\neq0$ in \eqref{eq:affine}---with the same paths, solvers, and
budgets. Eleven of eighteen blocks are inversions ($\mathcal{R}$ prefers
VP, margin $0.7513$; exact $\wtwo$ prefers linear), and all eleven pass
the 80-digit audit (max float64 deviation $2.07\times10^{-11}$; smallest
inverted margin $1.42\times10^{-6}$)
(\artifact{workshop\_external\_validation\_2026-07-24-v1:results}). This
supports the finding only for the tested systems. No mixture target
supports any conclusion here; mixture evidence was excluded after an
estimator-calibration failure.

\section{A minimal non-implication for fixed-grid Euler}
\label{sec:prop}

The benchmark shows the failure empirically; the following shows the
implication already fails logically, in one smooth scalar dimension.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:euler}
Fix $L>0$ and an integer $N\ge1$; let $h=1/N$ and let forward Euler use the
left-endpoint grid $t_n=nh$ on $[0,1]$. Set
$\epsilon_N=N\{e^{L/N}-1\}-L>0$ and define $f_k(t,x)=a_k(t)x$ with
$a_0(t)=L$ and $a_{1,N}(t)=L+\epsilon_N\cos(2\pi Nt)$. For $x(0)=1$:
(i) both exact endpoints equal $e^{L}$;
(ii) $\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\epsilon_N^2/2>\int_0^1 a_0^2$;
(iii) Euler is \emph{exact} for $a_{1,N}$;
(iv) Euler has strictly positive endpoint error for $a_0$.
Hence larger averaged squared Jacobian does not imply larger fixed-grid
Euler endpoint error.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\int_0^1\cos(2\pi Nt)\,dt=0$ for integer $N$, so both coefficients
integrate to $L$ and $x(1)=e^{L}$.
(ii) By orthogonality
$\int_0^1a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\epsilon_N^2/2$, and $\epsilon_N>0$ since
$e^{z}>1+z$ for $z=L/N>0$.
(iii) At every node $\cos(2\pi Nt_n)=1$, so each Euler factor is
$1+h(L+\epsilon_N)=e^{L/N}$ and the product is $e^{L}$.
(iv) $(1+L/N)^N<e^{L}$ since $\log(1+z)<z$ for $z>0$.
\end{proof}

The quantifiers matter: $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid, its
amplitude vanishes as $N\to\infty$, and shifted grids or multi-stage
solvers sample different phases. This elementary grid-aliasing
construction, which passed an independent adversarial review, establishes
a logical non-implication for fixed-grid left-endpoint Euler only; we
claim neither the mechanism behind every Gaussian inversion nor a new
numerical-analysis phenomenon.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and stochastic interpolants define the Gaussian path family
and its marginal fields~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,
liu2023rectified}; Lipschitz-guided schedule design proposes the criterion
we test~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}. Fast diffusion solvers exploit exactly
integrated linear parts and solver-specific
expansions~\citep{lu2022dpmsolver,lu2022dpmsolverpp,zhang2023deis}, EDM
separates schedule from discretization~\citep{karras2022edm}, and Align
Your Steps optimizes schedules per solver, model, and
dataset~\citep{sabour2024align}: schedule quality is known to be
solver-dependent. Runge--Kutta and backward error analysis supply the
local-error machinery~\citep{butcher1963coefficients,hairer1993solving,
hairer2006geometric}; Gaussian $\wtwo$ is due to
\citet{gelbrich1990formula}. None of these states the exact commuting
inversion benchmark or the grid-aware non-implication, though search
absence does not establish novelty.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}

Our systems are exact, commuting, affine Gaussian flows with two paths,
three solvers, dimensions 2 and 8, and small budgets, plus one
pre-registered non-centered family. No learned neural field is evaluated;
no image, video, or world-model improvement is demonstrated or implied; the
solver-specific proxy is post-hoc and in-sample; the proposition is
grid-aware by construction. Averaged regularity may remain valuable for
schedule design, and our result does not refute Lipschitz-guided schedules:
in the tested systems the averaged-regularity \emph{ordering} does not
determine the fixed-NFE $\wtwo$ ordering, because fixed-budget error lives
in solver-stage samples and transported signed defects invisible to an
unsigned time average. The benchmark is a small, auditable test bed for
schedule-selection criteria: every numerical statement carries a
checksummed artifact identifier whose manifest records commit, config and
environment hashes, and seeds; the anonymized audit bundle accompanies
submission.
