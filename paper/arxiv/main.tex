\begin{abstract}
Flow matching and stochastic interpolants specify a probability flow ODE
whose few-step sampling error depends on the sampling schedule. Schedule
design therefore needs an a priori criterion. Chen, Vanden-Eijnden, and Xu
propose minimizing the averaged squared Jacobian norm $A_2$, a Lipschitz
constant of the marginal field, as a selection criterion, without a proved
bound on discretization error. This paper asks whether the order induced by
$A_2$ between two paths is reliable at a fixed number of function
evaluations (NFE).

Already for centered Gaussian interpolants the implication fails. For
independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$ endpoints, the exact regularity integrals
of the linear and trigonometric variance-preserving paths are $5\pi/8-1$
and $\pi^2/16$. Explicit Heun method, a two-stage Runge--Kutta scheme, with
NFE $8$ reverses the ranking of Gaussian Wasserstein-2 distance: the linear
factor is the rational $6797469/3559400$, and a rational enclosure of the
VP product in $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$ yields $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$.

Three regimes then separate. As a pairwise comparator of linear versus VP,
the ranking inverts in $5$ of $12$ geometry-by-solver cells ($4$ of $12$ at
every tested NFE), from $3$ distinct regularity comparisons. As an
in-family objective, trigonometric VP versus the scalar log-covariance
schedule (Chen Example 3.3 with $M=\lambda_{\max}$) invert in $9$ of $36$
blocks and $4$ of $12$ cells. The unconstrained per-mode minimizer attains
both the smallest regularity and the smallest Wasserstein-2 distance in
$36$ of $36$ blocks, but is not a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for
$d\geq 2$. The analysis is confined to commuting Gaussians; no learned
field is used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A few-step sampler engages a probability path, a numerical solver, and
a budget of number of function evaluations (NFE) before any endpoint error
is observed. Few-step sampling therefore makes the sampling schedule a
design choice that must be made a priori. Classical one-step bounds involve a Lipschitz constant of
the field~\citep{hairer1993solving}. What the practitioner needs is a
scalar criterion for schedule design that can be evaluated from the path
alone.

In the stochastic interpolants and flow matching setting,
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing averaged squared Jacobian
regularity (their Definition~3.2, denoted $A_2$) as a selection criterion.
The integrand is a Jacobian norm. They prove KL equivalence of schedules
and a logarithmic scaling law. They prove no bound relating $A_2$ to
discretization error, Wasserstein-2 distance, or sampling error. This paper
asks whether the pairwise order that $A_2$ induces between two admissible
paths is reliable at equal NFE on a probability flow ODE.

Commuting Gaussian interpolants are the right probe. Both the regularity
integral $\cR$ and Gaussian $\wtwo$ are available in closed form, and the
regularity integrand is known exactly, so any observed divergence is a
property of the criterion rather than an estimation artefact. The family
includes the linear interpolant, trigonometric variance-preserving (VP)
path, and Chen et al.'s scalar log-covariance schedule, all shared
$(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolants, as well as the unconstrained per-mode
minimizer of $\cR$.

The leading logical result is a deterministic scalar example, proved by an
exact integral, an exact rational product of the Heun method, and a
rational enclosure of a two-stage Runge--Kutta product
(Section~\ref{subsec:counterexample}). The same calculation then splits
into three regimes, summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}: $\cR$ as a
pairwise comparator of two fixed paths, $\cR$ as an in-family objective,
and $\cR$ when its unconstrained minimizer is admissible.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A certified one-dimensional counterexample: exact $\cR$ prefers
  trigonometric VP while Heun at NFE~$8$ prefers the linear path in
  Gaussian $\wtwo$ (Section~\ref{subsec:counterexample},
  Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}). An exact integral or an exact Heun product
  that restored the $\cR$ ranking would contradict the claim.
\item An exact account of the information discarded by the unsigned
  average: leading exact-minus-method coefficients, stage locations, and
  a forced sign change of the linear drift
  (Section~\ref{subsec:discarded}). A truncation expansion in which those
  coefficients reduced to $\|A\|_2^2$ would contradict the account.
\item A three-regime characterisation on a complete $4\times 3\times 3$
  census: pairwise linear-versus-VP inversions in $5$ of $12$ cells;
  in-family VP-versus-scalar-log-covariance inversions in $9$ of $36$
  blocks; unconstrained per-mode minimizer smallest in $36$ of $36$
  blocks, and not a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$
  (Sections~\ref{subsec:pairwise}--\ref{subsec:minimizer}). A census in
  which all three comparisons agreed would contradict the split.
\item An inversion map over target variance
  $\lambda\in[0.05,100]$, reported as log-Lebesgue measure rather than a
  grid-point count (Section~\ref{subsec:region}). Emptiness of the
  inversion set on that interval would contradict the map.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Gaussian interpolants and the marginal velocity field}
\label{subsec:interpolants}

Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be independent
in $\RR^d$, with $\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1$ symmetric positive definite. Let
$\alpha,\sigma:[0,1]\to\RR$ be $C^3$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(0)=1,\quad \sigma(0)=0,\quad \alpha(1)=0,\quad \sigma(1)=1.
\end{equation}
The interpolant is
\begin{equation}
  X_t=\alpha(t)X_0+\sigma(t)X_1,
  \label{eq:interpolant}
\end{equation}
as in stochastic interpolants and flow matching~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Throughout, $\alpha^2+\sigma^2$ is not required to equal $1$ except on the VP
path and on Chen et al.'s scalar log-covariance schedule. The two classical
paths compared below are the linear schedule $(\alpha,\sigma)=(1-t,\,t)$ and
the trigonometric VP schedule $(\cos(\pi t/2),\,\sin(\pi t/2))$.

The law of $X_t$ is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and
covariance $Q(t)=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$. We assume $Q(t)\succ 0$
on $[0,1]$. The marginal velocity of~\eqref{eq:interpolant} is the affine
field
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A(t)=C_t Q(t)^{-1},\qquad
  c(t)=\dot m_t-A(t)m_t,
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with $C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$. This is a
marginal interpolant ODE, not a score-based probability flow ODE in the sense
of~\citet{song2021score}, although the latter is an instance of a generative
ODE. If $\mu_0=\mu_1=0$ and $\Sigma_0=I$, then $c\equiv 0$ and
$A=\tfrac12 Q'Q^{-1}$.

If $\Sigma_0=I$, then $Q(t)$, $Q'(t)$, $A(t)$, and $\Sigma_1$ share the
eigenbasis of $\Sigma_1$. Simultaneous diagonalization with the identity is
not an extra hypothesis. In that basis, mode $i$ with eigenvalue
$\lambda_i>0$ obeys $x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with
$q_i(t)=\alpha(t)^2+\lambda_i\sigma(t)^2$ and
$a_i(t)=q_i'(t)/(2q_i(t))$. The covariance drift depends only on the
variance schedule. The exact one-step factor on $[t_n,t_{n+1}]$ is
$\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$. For centered modes the exact endpoint factor
is $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

\subsection{Solvers, evaluation budget, and endpoint error}
\label{subsec:solvers}

Euler uses $s=1$ field evaluation per step, Heun $s=2$, and classical RK4
$s=4$. A budget $B$ is admitted only if $B\equiv 0\pmod s$. The step count
is $S=B/s$ and the grid is the uniform partition of $[0,1]$ with $S$ steps.
Equal NFE is not equal step count: at $B=8$, Euler takes $8$ steps, Heun $4$,
and RK4 $2$.

A solver applied to~\eqref{eq:affine} produces an affine endpoint map
$x\mapsto Rx+d$. Source moments are pushed through this map. Error is the
Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint \emph{laws}~\citep{gelbrich1990formula,peyre2019computational},
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^2=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2
  +\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\hat C+\Sigma_1-2(\Sigma_1^{1/2}\hat C\Sigma_1^{1/2})^{1/2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is neither pathwise trajectory error nor local truncation error. In the
centered commuting case, $\wtwo^2=\sum_i\bigl(|r_i|-\sqrt{\lambda_i}\bigr)^2$.

\subsection{The averaged squared Jacobian criterion}
\label{subsec:criterion}

\citet[Definition~3.2]{lipschitzguided2025} introduce
\begin{equation}
  A_2=\int_0^1 \mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\|\nabla b_t(I_t)\bigr\|_2^2\bigr]\,dt.
\end{equation}
For the affine field~\eqref{eq:affine} the integrand is deterministic and
\begin{equation}
  \cR[b]=\int_0^1 \bigl\|A(t)\bigr\|_2^2\,dt
  \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
coincides with $A_2$. By definition $\cR$ ignores $c(t)$. For commuting
$A(t)$, $\|A(t)\|_2=\max_i |a_i(t)|$. Headline comparisons use the continuous
integral~\eqref{eq:R}. The historical $24$-node trapezoidal estimator is
written $\Rhat$ and is used only as an implementation check. No trapezoidal
value is described as an exact integral.

\paragraph{What the criterion does and does not establish.}
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose $A_2$ as a design objective. They prove
that schedules related by a time-change have equivalent KL cost, and that
the optimized one-dimensional log-covariance schedule attains a logarithmic
scaling of $A_2$. They prove no bound relating $A_2$ to a discretization
error, a Wasserstein error, or a sampling error. They never compare the
linear interpolant to trigonometric VP.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The variance-path invariant and the Cauchy--Schwarz bound}
\label{subsec:invariant}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:cs}
For any admissible one-dimensional variance path from $1$ to $\lambda>0$,
$\int_0^1 a(t)\,dt=\tfrac12\ln\lambda$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \cR=\int_0^1 a(t)^2\,dt
  =\Bigl(\int_0^1 a(t)\,dt\Bigr)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)
  \ge \bigl(\tfrac12\ln\lambda\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $a$ is constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integral identity is $\tfrac12\log q(1)-\tfrac12\log q(0)$. Expanding
$\int a^2$ as $(\int a)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)$ gives the bound.
\end{proof}

Thus $\cR$ measures only the temporal variance of the log-covariance rate.
Its unconstrained minimizer is the constant drift $a\equiv\tfrac12\ln\lambda$,
i.e.\ $q(t)=\lambda^t$, which is \citet[Example~3.3]{lipschitzguided2025} in
one dimension. In several dimensions the per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$
attains $\cR=\bigl(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|\bigr)^2$. That schedule is
not a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$.

\subsection{A certified one-dimensional counterexample}
\label{subsec:counterexample}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:scalar}
The endpoints $X_0\sim N(0,1)$ and $X_1\sim N(0,4)$ are independent.
The compared schedules are linear and trigonometric VP. The solver is
explicit Heun with NFE $B=8$, hence $S=4$ steps of size $h=1/4$.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:scalar}, $q(t)=\alpha(t)^2+4\sigma(t)^2$ and
$a(t)=q'(t)/(2q(t))$. The exact endpoint factor is $2$. All Heun factors
below are positive, so $\wtwo=\bigl||r|-2\bigr|=|r-2|$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:scalar}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:scalar},
\begin{align}
  \cR_{\mathrm{lin}}&=\frac{5\pi}{8}-1,\\
  \cR_{\mathrm{VP}}&=\frac{\pi^2}{16},
\end{align}
so averaged regularity prefers VP. The Heun endpoint factors satisfy
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{6797469}{3559400},\qquad
  0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}<\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
the second bound being a rational enclosure obtained by substituting
$\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ into the exact element of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{321331}{3559400}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}},
\end{equation}
so equal-NFE Gaussian $\wtwo$ prefers the linear path. The regularity
ordering does not determine the fixed-NFE $\wtwo$ ordering.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear drift is $a(t)=(5t-1)/(1-2t+5t^2)$. Completing the square to
$5(t-1/5)^2+4/5$ and using $\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2)=\pi/2$ yields
$\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$.
The VP integral evaluates to $\pi^2/16$. The four linear Heun factors are
the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, whose product is
$6797469/3559400$. Then
$321331/3559400<91/1000$ is an elementary comparison of integers.
The VP grid drifts are exact elements of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; the
four-step Heun product has only nonnegative coefficients, so substituting
the rational upper bounds $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ is valid and
gives $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$, hence $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
Details occupy Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}.
\end{proof}

One certified inversion proves the logical non-implication. The remainder of
the paper identifies what $\cR$ cannot retain, then measures how the failure
depends on the regime in which $\cR$ is used.

\subsection{What the unsigned average discards}
\label{subsec:discarded}

The content of a ranking inversion is the leading exact-minus-method
coefficient of the one-step factor (Appendix~\ref{app:solvers}):
$\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for Euler and
$\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3$ for the implemented Heun factor
$1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t)+a(t+h)(1+ha(t))\bigr)$. These involve different
derivatives, signs, and stage locations. Averaging $\|A\|_2^2$ retains none
of them.

The telescoping identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n=0}^{S-1} r_{i,n}-\prod_{n=0}^{S-1} e_{i,n}
  =\sum_{n=0}^{S-1}(r_{i,n}-e_{i,n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
is the accounting tool that converts those local coefficients into endpoint
factor error. Here $e_{i,n}=\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$ is exact and
$r_{i,n}$ is numerical. Prefixes are numerical; suffixes are exact. The
identity holds without a non-vanishing hypothesis. Appendix~\ref{app:tables}
illustrates the signed transported terms on the strongest centered block.

The structural distinction that every fixed-stage Runge--Kutta method
samples, and that $\cR$ erases, is a sign change. For any independent linear
coupling, $a_{\mathrm{lin}}(0)=-1<0<a_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)=+1$. The VP drift
vanishes at the endpoints and does not change sign on $(0,1)$.

\subsection{Three regimes: comparator, in-family objective, unconstrained minimiser}
\label{subsec:regimes}

Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} is a pairwise comparison of two fixed paths.
Using $\cR$ as a design objective is a different question, and admitting its
unconstrained minimizer is a third. The rest of the paper answers these
three questions on one factorial grid.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item $\cR$ as a \emph{pairwise comparator} between two arbitrary admissible
  paths (linear versus trigonometric VP).
\item $\cR$ as an \emph{in-family objective}: both paths are shared
  $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolants, namely trigonometric VP and the scalar
  Chen Example~3.3 schedule with $M=\lambda_{\max}$,
  $\sigma^2(t)=(M^t-1)/(M-1)$ and $\alpha^2=1-\sigma^2$.
\item $\cR$ as a criterion when its \emph{unconstrained minimizer} is
  admissible. The per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ attains
  $\bigl(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|\bigr)^2$ and is not a shared
  $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$.
\end{enumerate}

A complementary scalar construction (Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1}) shows
non-implication for left-endpoint Euler on a grid-aware oscillatory field.
That field is chosen after the grid. It is not the mechanism of
Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} or of the Gaussian census.

\subsection{Statistical treatment and what the counts mean}
\label{subsec:stats}

Almost every quantity in this paper is deterministic. Given a geometry, a
path, a solver, and a budget, $\cR$ and $\wtwo$ are exact reals computed in
float64 and checked at $80$ digits. There is no sampling noise on those
cells. A hypothesis test, a standard-error interval, or a confidence
interval on $\cR$, on $\wtwo$, or on a census count such as ``$5$ of $12$''
would not be an inference: it would confuse a calculation with a sample.
Five instruments replace that confusion.

First, the $36$-block grid is a complete enumeration of the factorial plan
$4$ geometries $\times$ $3$ solvers $\times$ $3$ budgets, not a draw. The
counts are census counts.

Second, the dependence is nested. $\cR$ depends on neither solver nor
budget, so its preference is constant on each geometry. The anisotropic
geometries $d=2$ and $d=8$ share the $\cR$ pair $(\numRlinAniso,\numRvpAniso)$
because the spectral norm retains only the extreme eigenvalues. The census
therefore contains \nDistinctRComparisons{} distinct linear-versus-VP $\cR$
comparisons, and the $36$ rows nest into \nGeometrySolverCells{}
geometry$\times$solver cells. Body values below are three-decimal displays
of the float64 integrals; the exact digits are in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}.

Third, each inversion is reported by a separation ratio
$\mathrm{margin}/\mathrm{floor}$, where the floor is the maximum observed
gap between float64 $\wtwo$ and the $80$-digit reference
($\numPrecFloor$). The pre-declared threshold is
$\ge\separationThreshold$. Every inversion in the census lies above it;
the smallest linear-versus-VP margin is $\precRatio$ times the floor.

Fourth, the only random ingredient, and the only legitimate inference, is
the draw of the low-rank factor $F$ (Section~\ref{subsec:robust-geometry}).

Fifth, Kendall $\tau$ between the $\cR$-induced order and the $\wtwo$-induced
order is reported as a descriptive statistic on a census, not an inferential
test. On the $36$ linear-versus-VP blocks, $\tau=\kendallTauCensus$
(Euler $\kendallTauEuler$, Heun $\kendallTauHeun$, RK4 $\kendallTauRK$).

\subsection{R as a pairwise comparator: linear versus VP}
\label{subsec:pairwise}

Does $\cR[b]<\cR[b']$ imply $E[b]<E[b']$ at equal NFE for the linear and

prefers VP ($\numWlinStrong$ versus $\numWvpStrong$). Appendix~\ref{app:tables}

frequency, and it does not speak to learned fields.

\subsection{Numerical validity controls}
\label{subsec:controls}

Adaptive quadrature of $\max_i a_i(t)^2$ agrees in sign with $\Rhat$ on
every headline geometry, including the scalar pair of
Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}. Trapezoidal estimates at $24$, $48$, $96$,
and $192$ nodes converge to the same ordering (Appendix~\ref{app:quadrature}).
All $72$ float64 $\wtwo$ values on the centered census agree with an
$80$-digit reference to at most $\numPrecFloor$. Interval procedures for
the VP Heun product occupy Appendix~\ref{app:quadrature}.

A pre-specified non-centered family (Appendix~\ref{app:noncentered}) shifts
the means so that $c(t)\ne 0$. Then $\cR$, which ignores $c$, is blind to a
mean contribution in $\wtwo^2$. Of $18$ blocks, $11$ invert under continuous
$\cR$, and several inversions including all dimension-$8$ Euler and Heun
inversions are mean-driven. The family is a structural stress test of that
blind spot, not an independent replication of the centered mechanism.

Mixture targets are excluded. Dimension-$8$ mixture endpoint estimators
failed a post-result calibration diagnostic; the census accepts Gaussian
targets only.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and stochastic interpolants supply the Gaussian path
family~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Lipschitz-guided schedule design proposes the selection criterion tested
here~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}. Solver-specific schedules and exact linear
parts are documented in diffusion practice~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm,lu2022dpmsolver,lu2022dpmsolverpp,zhang2023deis}.

\citet{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} prove sharp Lipschitz regularity of
canonical flow-matching fields and Euler-type $\wtwo$ rates of order
$\sqrt{d}/N$ up to logs. That is a bound, not a pairwise ranking of two
interpolants at equal NFE. \citet{gupta2026sharpen} allocate Euler steps
where a learned field is sharp; the path is held fixed. \citet{yuan2026instance}
learn instance-aware grids. \citet{du2026lagrangian} select paths by a
least-action principle. \citet{khan2026isofm} regularizes the material derivative
of learned fields.

\citet{tao2026strain} decompose $\nabla v=S+\Omega$ and control Euler error
by the logarithmic norm $\mu_2=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, which is signed, whereas
$\cR$ integrates $\|A\|_2^2$. In one dimension $\mu_2(a)=a$ and
$\|A\|_2=|a|$. Their Theorem~5 states that McCann displacement interpolation
has vanishing material derivative, hence Euler is of order $2$ in the
Eulerian analysis; Remark~7 records the stronger Lagrangian exactness
observed on that interpolant. Neither statement applies to the
independent-coupling linear or VP path studied here. The present paper
supplies a rationally certified Gaussian instance of ranking failure for
the unsigned average $\|A\|_2^2$, together with a three-regime map of when
that scalar remains a reliable objective on this family. A counterexample to
$\mu_2$ itself is outside our scope.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Averaged squared Jacobian regularity does not determine the equal-NFE
Gaussian $\wtwo$ ranking of two fixed interpolants. The fact is proved by a
one-dimensional Heun calculation whose integrals and linear factor are exact
and whose VP factor is enclosed by a rational substitution in
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$.

The same scalar splits into three regimes. As a pairwise comparator of
linear versus VP it inverts on a complete census. As an in-family objective
comparing two shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolants it still inverts. When
its unconstrained minimizer is admitted, that minimizer attains the smallest
$\cR$ and the smallest $\wtwo$ in $36$ of $36$ blocks; it is not a shared
$(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant for $d\geq 2$.

On this commuting Gaussian family, $\cR$ is usable as an objective to
minimize whenever its minimizer is attainable, and is not usable to rank two
fixed paths.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The systems are commuting affine Gaussian interpolants. No learned neural
field, image, video, or world-model quantity is reported. The solvers are
three fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods at small equal-NFE budgets.
Exponential integrators solve affine scalar modes exactly, so the
comparisons concern generic RK discretizations of those modes rather than
the best integrator for an affine field. The Euler construction of
Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1} depends on the grid: the field is chosen after the
grid. Extended robustness grids (NFE $64$ and $128$, $\pm 10\%$ noise-floor
perturbations) are post-hoc. Census counts are hierarchical enumerations,
not population frequencies. No counterexample to the signed logarithmic
norm $\mu_2$ is provided.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}}
\label{app:scalar}

\paragraph{Linear drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(1-t)^2+4t^2=1-2t+5t^2$ and
$q'= -2+10t$, so
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(5t-1)/q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)$. Completing the square yields the two equivalent identities
\begin{align}
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\Bigl[(t-\tfrac15)^2+\bigl(\tfrac25\bigr)^2\Bigr],
  \label{eq:q-bracket}\\
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\bigl(t-\tfrac15\bigr)^2+\tfrac45.
  \label{eq:q-plus}
\end{align}
Thus
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(t-1/5)/\bigl((t-1/5)^2+(2/5)^2\bigr)$.
The substitution $u=t-1/5=(2/5)\tan\theta$ gives
$du=(2/5)\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and converts $\int a_{\mathrm{lin}}^2$ into
$(5/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. The definite limits are
$\theta(0)=\arctan(-1/2)$ and $\theta(1)=\arctan 2$. The antiderivative
$(5/4)(\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta)$ therefore contributes
$(5/4)(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))-(5/4)(4/5)$. Both angles lie in
$(0,\pi/2)$ and $\tan(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))$ is undefined, so the sum is
$\pi/2$. Hence $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$. (The subtracted $1$ is the
boundary evaluation of $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\tan\theta/(1+\tan^2\theta)$.)

\paragraph{VP drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\cos^2(\pi t/2)+4\sin^2(\pi t/2)=(5-3\cos(\pi t))/2$ and
$q'=(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\frac{(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)}{5-3\cos(\pi t)}
  =\frac{(3\pi/4)\sin(\pi t)}{q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)}.
\end{equation}
The change of variables $u=\pi t$ yields
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=(9\pi/4)\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du$. The
standard Weierstrass evaluation of the integrand gives
$\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du=\pi/36$, hence
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\pi^2/16$. Both closed forms were checked by adaptive
quadrature (absolute discrepancy below $10^{-12}$; quadrature error
estimates are not used as certificates).

\paragraph{Heun recurrence.}
On $x'=a(t)x$ the implemented Heun factor is
$r=1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t_n)+a(t_{n+1})(1+h a(t_n))\bigr)$. With $h=1/4$ the four
linear factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, all
positive, with product $6797469/3559400$. Then $2-r=321331/3559400$, and
$321331\cdot 1000<91\cdot 3559400$ is an integer comparison, so
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091$.

\paragraph{Exact VP grid and rational certificate.}
The VP drift is
$a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)/(5-3\cos(\pi t))$. Evaluating at the
Heun nodes gives
\begin{align}
  a(0)&=0,\\
  a(1/4)&=\frac{(9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82},\\
  a(1/2)&=\frac{3\pi}{10},\\
  a(3/4)&=\frac{(-9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82},\\
  a(1)&=0.
\end{align}
Positivity of $a(3/4)$ follows from $15\sqrt{2}>9$, or $15^2\cdot 2-9^2=369>0$
after squaring. Each Heun factor is therefore strictly positive, so
$0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}=2-r_{\mathrm{VP}}$.
The four-step product is an element $P(\pi,\sqrt{2})$ of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. Expanding and reducing $\sqrt{2}^2=2$ produces
twelve monomials, all with nonnegative coefficients. Substituting any pair of
upper bounds for $\pi$ and $\sqrt{2}$ is therefore valid.
The bound $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ is the integer comparison $99^2-2\cdot 70^2=1$.
The bound $\pi<355/113$ follows from Machin's formula
$\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$ together with Leibniz remainders: seven
Taylor terms upper-bound $\arctan(1/5)$ and four terms lower-bound
$\arctan(1/239)$. The resulting rational is strictly less than $355/113$ by
an integer comparison. Substituting these enclosures yields
\begin{equation}
  P\Bigl(\frac{355}{113},\frac{99}{70}\Bigr)
  =\frac{\vpHeunBoundNum}{\vpHeunBoundDen}
  <\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
equivalently the integer comparison
\begin{equation}
  \vpHeunIntLeft<\vpHeunIntRight.
\end{equation}
Hence $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
An independent interval enclosure at $40$ decimal digits and an $80$-digit
non-interval product agree with this bound and with the float64
implementation to $10^{-14}$. They are cross-checks, not the proof.

\section{Solver formulas and local truncation expansions}
\label{app:solvers}

On $[t,t+h]$ for $x'=a(t)x$,
\begin{align}
  r_{\mathrm{Euler}}&=1+ha(t),\\
  r_{\mathrm{Heun}}&=1+\tfrac h2\bigl(a(t)+a(t+h)(1+ha(t))\bigr),
\end{align}
and classical RK4 with stages at $t$, $t+h/2$ (twice), and $t+h$. Equal NFE
requires $B\equiv 0\pmod s$ and sets $S=B/s$.

Assume $a$ is $C^3$. The exact factor admits
$1+ha+(h^2/2)(a'+a^2)+(h^3/6)(a''+3aa'+a^3)+O(h^4)$. Subtracting Euler
leaves $\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2+O(h^3)$. Substituting $a(t+h)$ into the
implemented Heun factor leaves $\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3+O(h^4)$. The
$aa'$ terms cancel. The expansion was confirmed with independent symbolic
algebra on the implemented expression.

\section{Grid-aware Euler construction}
\label{app:p4p1}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:euler}
Fix $L>0$ and an integer $N\ge 1$. Let $h=1/N$ and let forward Euler use the
left-endpoint grid $t_n=nh$ on $[0,1]$. Set
$\varepsilon_N=N(e^{L/N}-1)-L>0$ and define $a_0(t)=L$ and
$a_{1,N}(t)=L+\varepsilon_N\cos(2\pi Nt)$. For $x(0)=1$: both exact
endpoints equal $e^{L}$; $\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2>\int_0^1 a_0^2$;
Euler is exact for $a_{1,N}$; Euler has strictly positive endpoint error for
$a_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The solution of $x'=a(t)x$, $x(0)=1$, is
$x(t)=\exp\bigl(\int_0^t a(s)\,ds\bigr)$. The cosine perturbation has
integer period, so $\int_0^1\cos(2\pi Nt)\,dt=0$ and both exact endpoints
equal $e^{L}$. Expanding the square and using the same orthogonality gives
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2$. The inequality
$\varepsilon_N>0$ is $e^z>1+z$ at $z=L/N>0$, hence
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2>\int_0^1 a_0^2=L^2$.

On the left-endpoint Euler grid, $\cos(2\pi Nt_n)=\cos(2\pi n)=1$, so
$1+h a_{1,N}(t_n)=1+(L+\varepsilon_N)/N=e^{L/N}$. Every oscillatory Euler
factor equals $e^{L/N}$ and the product is $e^{L}$. For the constant field
every Euler factor equals $1+L/N$, and the product is $(1+L/N)^N$. Since
$\log(1+z)<z$ for $z=L/N>0$, one has $(1+L/N)^N<e^{L}$.
\end{proof}

The field $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid. The construction is not the
mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}. An $80$-digit check confirms that
the oscillatory Euler product equals $e^{L}$, but it is not a substitute for
the argument above.

\section{Non-centered structural stress test}
\label{app:noncentered}

The quantity $\cR$ depends only on $A(t)$. If $c(t)\ne 0$, the mean still
contributes $\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ to $\wtwo^2$. Dimensions $d\in\{2,8\}$,
coordinates indexed by $i=0,\ldots,d-1$. Source $N(\mu_0,I)$ with
$\mu_{0,i}=0.75(-1)^i$. Target $N(\mu_1,D)$ with $\mu_{1,i}=1+0.25\,i$ and
$D=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$, where the $\lambda_i$ form a geometric
sequence from $6^{-1/2}$ to $6^{1/2}$ (condition number $6$). Then
$c(t)\ne 0$. Paths are linear and trigonometric VP. Solvers are Euler, Heun,
and RK4 at NFE $B\in\{8,16,32\}$. Write $M=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ and
$C=\wtwo^2-M$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}=M_{\mathrm{lin}}-M_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}=C_{\mathrm{lin}}-C_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{total}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\end{equation}
A block is mean-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$
and $|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|$, and
covariance-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$ and
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|$. Otherwise it is mixed.
The same paths, solvers, and budgets give \nWorkshopBlocks{} blocks, of
which \nWorkshopInversions{} invert under continuous $\cR$.
Table~\ref{tab:workshop} reports the driver of each path preference.

\section{Quadrature, precision, and interval procedures}
\label{app:quadrature}

Table~\ref{tab:quad} compares the continuous integral with the historical
$24$-node trapezoidal estimator on every headline geometry. Path orderings
agree. Trapezoidal values at $24$, $48$, $96$, and $192$ nodes converge to
the same sign. Adaptive quadrature is used for multi-mode $\cR$; the scalar
integrals of Appendix~\ref{app:scalar} are closed form. Scipy error
estimates are not interval certificates.

Scalar linear Heun factors are computed in exact rationals. The VP Heun
product is certified by substituting $\pi<355/113$ and $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ into
a nonnegative element of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; the ranking inequality
is the integer comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}. An independent
interval enclosure at $40$ decimal digits
(\texttt{mpmath}~\mpmathVersion) and an $80$-digit (non-interval) product
are recorded as cross-checks against the float64 Heun implementation.
Frozen Gaussian $\wtwo$ values on the centered census were checked against
an $80$-digit Gelbrich reference. Default numerical precision for the
benchmark runs is float64.

linear-versus-VP inversion set on $[0.05,100]$. The declared resolution is
\logLebesgueNodes{} log-uniform nodes; doubling to $2561$ nodes changes
every cell by less than $1\%$.

\section{Robustness grids}
\label{app:robust}

The post-hoc low-rank grid has $66$ preference blocks:
$2$ dimensions, $3$ solvers, and, for each pair, the three primary NFE
budgets $\{8,16,32\}$ at noise-floor perturbations
$\delta\in\{-0.1,0,0.1\}$, plus NFE $\{64,128\}$ at $\delta=0$ only. The
perturbation multiplies the noise variance $0.05$ by $(1+\delta)$; the
factor $F$ is regenerated from seed $271828$ and is not redrawn. Extra
budgets are not run at $\delta\ne 0$. Table~\ref{tab:robust} summarizes the
$66$ blocks. All $66$ follow the same solver-path split as the primary
low-rank census: VP under Euler, linear under Heun and RK4.
