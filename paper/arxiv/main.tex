\begin{abstract}
Few-step sampling in flow matching and stochastic interpolants requires
choosing an interpolation schedule before endpoint error is observed.
Averaged squared Lipschitzness of the drift, measured by the
time-integrated squared $2$-norm of its spatial Jacobian, is a plausible
schedule-design criterion, but it is not a solver-specific
discretization-error functional. Chen, Vanden-Eijnden, and Xu propose
minimizing this quantity, denoted $A_2$; they do not claim or prove that
$A_2$ universally ranks equal-NFE solver error. We study that narrower
surrogate question. For independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$, the exact
regularity integrals are $5\pi/8-1$ and $\pi^2/16$, so regularity prefers
trigonometric VP, whereas explicit Heun, a two-stage Runge--Kutta method,
with a budget of eight function evaluations (NFE~$8$), prefers the linear
path in Gaussian Wasserstein-2 distance. The inversion is certified by
an exact rational Heun product and a nonnegative element of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. A complementary construction shows that, for
every step count $N$ and every admissible endpoint log-scale, the unique
integrated-regularity minimizer can have strictly larger $N$-step Euler
endpoint error than a higher-regularity competitor aligned with that
solver grid. A specified finite Gaussian enumeration of four candidate
paths on $36$ tested blocks contains both agreement and pairwise
disagreement. These results limit a universal surrogate interpretation;
they do not evaluate learned velocity fields or estimate a population
failure frequency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A few-step sampler commits to a probability path, a numerical solver, and a
function-evaluation budget before any endpoint error is observed.
Few-step sampling therefore makes the sampling schedule a design choice
that must be made a priori. Classical one-step bounds
involve a Lipschitz constant of the field~\citep{hairer1993solving}. A
scalar functional of the path that can be evaluated before discretization
is an attractive surrogate.

In the stochastic interpolants and flow matching setting,
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing averaged squared
Lipschitzness of the drift (their Definition~3.2, denoted $A_2$) as a
schedule-design objective. They prove KL equivalence of scalar schedules after a
posteriori diffusion tuning, a transfer formula, and a logarithmic
scaling law for the one-dimensional log-covariance minimizer. They
minimize $A_2$ in specified admissible families. They do not claim, and
do not prove, a universal pairwise implication of the form
\begin{equation}
  R(q_1)<R(q_2)
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{solver},N}(q_1)
  <
  \operatorname{Err}_{\mathrm{solver},N}(q_2).
\end{equation}
The present paper studies that narrower ranking question on
flow-matching marginal interpolant ODEs, not score-based probability-flow
ODEs.

The object is an integrated unsigned Jacobian-energy functional. For a
fixed finite-step solver, endpoint error depends on stagewise field
values, temporal derivatives, quadrature structure, and signed
cancellations that are not determined by that energy alone.

Already for independent $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$, exact regularity integrals
prefer trigonometric VP while explicit Heun at NFE~$8$ prefers the linear
path in Gaussian $\wtwo$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}). The comparison
uses exact $W_2$ between Gaussians and a fully certified scalar
calculation.

For every step count $N$ and every admissible endpoint log-scale $L>0$,
a solver-grid-aware Gaussian construction exhibits two smooth scalar
paths with identical endpoints such that the unique minimizer of
integrated squared field energy has strictly larger $N$-step Euler
endpoint error than a higher-regularity competitor
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware}). The competitor is constructed as a
function of the solver resolution and exploits exact grid alignment. It
is not offered as a model of naturally occurring or learned schedules.

A specified finite Gaussian enumeration then delimits where pairwise
disagreement appears and where it does not
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Among four specified candidate paths,
the per-mode log-covariance path has both the smallest $\cR$ and the
smallest $\wtwo$ in $36$ of $36$ tested blocks. Pairwise linear-versus-VP
and VP-versus-scalar-log-covariance comparisons on those tested blocks
contain inversions. These counts are a finite enumeration, not a
population frequency and not a global optimality statement.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The analysis uses commuting affine Gaussian interpolants, three
fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods, and small equal-NFE budgets. No learned
neural velocity field is evaluated. The paper does not claim that
average Jacobian regularity is generally useless, that it is empirically
inferior in typical flow-matching training, that Chen et al.'s
optimization results are invalid, or that ranking inversions are
frequent in practice. Signed information is consistent with the
certified inversion; it is not isolated as a unique causal source.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A certified one-dimensional Gaussian ranking inversion: exact
  $\cR$ prefers trigonometric VP while Heun at NFE~$8$ prefers the linear
  path in Gaussian $\wtwo$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar},
  Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}).
\item Exact one-dimensional identities and solver-specific local
  truncation expansions for the implemented Euler and Heun factors
  (Section~\ref{sec:local}, Appendix~\ref{app:solvers}).
\item A solver-grid-aware impossibility theorem: the unique integrated
  squared-energy minimizer need not minimize fixed-grid Euler endpoint
  error (Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware}).
\item A finite Gaussian enumeration of four specified candidate paths on
  $36$ tested blocks, containing both agreement and pairwise disagreement
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Definitions and standing assumptions}
\label{sec:definitions}

Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be independent
in $\RR^d$, with $\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1$ symmetric positive definite. A
\emph{shared schedule} is a pair of scalar functions used for every
eigenmode, equivalently $Q(t)=\alpha(t)^2\Sigma_0+\sigma(t)^2\Sigma_1$.
We distinguish three regularity classes.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \emph{Class~S} (shared $C^1$ interpolants): $\alpha,\sigma\in C^1([0,1])$
  with
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(0)=1,\quad \sigma(0)=0,\quad \alpha(1)=0,\quad \sigma(1)=1,
\end{equation}
  and $(\alpha(t),\sigma(t))\neq(0,0)$ for every $t\in[0,1]$. The linear
  path $(1-t,\,t)$ and trigonometric VP
  $(\cos(\pi t/2),\,\sin(\pi t/2))$ belong to Class~S, as does the
  grid-aware embedding of Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1}.
\item \emph{Class~Q} (regular covariance paths): a $C^1$ path of positive
  definite covariances $Q(t)$ with $Q(0)=\Sigma_0$, $Q(1)=\Sigma_1$, for
  which $A(t)=\tfrac12 Q'(t)Q(t)^{-1}$ extends continuously to $[0,1]$.
  Every Class~S interpolant induces a Class~Q path, because the
  simultaneous-zero prohibition and $\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1\succ 0$ give
  $Q(t)\succ 0$ on $[0,1]$. The per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ is
  Class~Q.
\item \emph{Class~R} (regular squares): $\alpha^2,\sigma^2\in C^1([0,1])$,
  $\alpha^2\ge 0$, $\sigma^2\ge 0$, and
  \begin{equation}
    \alpha(0)^2=1,\quad \sigma(0)^2=0,\quad \alpha(1)^2=0,\quad \sigma(1)^2=1,
  \end{equation}
  with $\alpha(t)^2+\sigma(t)^2>0$ on $[0,1]$. Square roots need not be
  differentiable at the endpoints. Chen et al.'s scalar log-covariance
  schedule $\sigma(t)^2=(M^t-1)/(M-1)$,
  $\alpha(t)^2=(M-M^t)/(M-1)$ for $M>0$, $M\neq 1$ is Class~R:
  $\sigma(t)\asymp\sqrt{t}$ as $t\to 0$ and $\alpha(t)\asymp\sqrt{1-t}$ as
  $t\to 1$, so it is not Class~S. When $M=1$ the formulas are read in the
  limit $\sigma(t)^2=t$, $\alpha(t)^2=1-t$. The covariance is $C^\infty$
  and $A$ is continuous, so the path is Class~Q.
\end{itemize}
Two paths are called shared-schedule Class~Q paths when both lie in
Class~Q and both use a single scalar pair $(\alpha,\sigma)$. We do not
call a Class~R path a Class~S interpolant.

The interpolant of a Class~S or Class~R schedule is
\begin{equation}
  X_t=\alpha(t)X_0+\sigma(t)X_1,
  \label{eq:interpolant}
\end{equation}
as in stochastic interpolants and flow matching~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Throughout, $\alpha^2+\sigma^2$ is not required to equal $1$ except on the VP
path and on Chen et al.'s scalar log-covariance schedule. The two classical
paths compared below are the linear schedule $(\alpha,\sigma)=(1-t,\,t)$ and
the trigonometric VP schedule $(\cos(\pi t/2),\,\sin(\pi t/2))$.

The law of $X_t$ is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and
covariance $Q(t)=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$. We assume $Q(t)\succ 0$
on $[0,1]$. The marginal velocity of~\eqref{eq:interpolant} is the affine
field
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A(t)=C_t Q(t)^{-1},\qquad
  c(t)=\dot m_t-A(t)m_t,
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with
$C_t=\tfrac12(\alpha^2)'\,\Sigma_0+\tfrac12(\sigma^2)'\,\Sigma_1$, which
extends continuously to $[0,1]$ whenever $\alpha^2,\sigma^2\in C^1([0,1])$.
On $(0,1)$ this coincides with
$C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$ wherever
$\alpha$ and $\sigma$ are differentiable and nonzero. This is a
marginal interpolant ODE, not a score-based probability-flow ODE in the sense
of~\citet{song2021score}. If $\mu_0=\mu_1=0$ and $\Sigma_0=I$, then $c\equiv 0$
and $A=\tfrac12 Q'Q^{-1}$.

If $\Sigma_0=I$, then $Q(t)$, $Q'(t)$, $A(t)$, and $\Sigma_1$ share the
eigenbasis of $\Sigma_1$. In that basis, mode $i$ with eigenvalue
$\lambda_i>0$ obeys $x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with
$q_i(t)=\alpha(t)^2+\lambda_i\sigma(t)^2$ and
$a_i(t)=q_i'(t)/(2q_i(t))$. For a Class~Q path that is not shared, the
same identity holds modewise with no single pair $(\alpha,\sigma)$. The
exact one-step factor on $[t_n,t_{n+1}]$ is
$\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$. For centered modes the exact endpoint factor
is $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

Euler uses $s=1$ field evaluation per step, Heun $s=2$, and classical RK4
$s=4$. A budget $B$ is admitted only if $B\equiv 0\pmod s$. The step count
is $S=B/s$ and the grid is the uniform partition of $[0,1]$ with $S$ steps.
Equal NFE is not equal step count: at $B=8$, Euler takes $8$ steps, Heun $4$,
and RK4 $2$. A solver applied to~\eqref{eq:affine} produces an affine
endpoint map $x\mapsto Rx+d$. Error is the Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint
laws~\citep{gelbrich1990formula,peyre2019computational},
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^2=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2
  +\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\hat C+\Sigma_1-2(\Sigma_1^{1/2}\hat C\Sigma_1^{1/2})^{1/2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
In the centered commuting case,
$\wtwo^2=\sum_i\bigl(|r_i|-\sqrt{\lambda_i}\bigr)^2$.

\citet[Definition~3.2]{lipschitzguided2025} introduce
\begin{equation}
  A_2=\int_0^1 \mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl\|\nabla b_t(I_t)\bigr\|_2^2\bigr]\,dt.
\end{equation}
For the affine field~\eqref{eq:affine} the integrand is deterministic and
\begin{equation}
  \cR[b]=\int_0^1 \bigl\|A(t)\bigr\|_2^2\,dt
  \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
coincides with $A_2$. By definition $\cR$ ignores $c(t)$. For commuting
$A(t)$, $\|A(t)\|_2=\max_i |a_i(t)|$. Headline comparisons use the continuous
integral~\eqref{eq:R}. The historical $24$-node trapezoidal estimator is
written $\Rhat$ and is used only as an implementation check.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:cs}
For any Class~Q one-dimensional variance path from $1$ to $\lambda>0$,
$\int_0^1 a(t)\,dt=\tfrac12\ln\lambda$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \cR=\int_0^1 a(t)^2\,dt
  =\Bigl(\int_0^1 a(t)\,dt\Bigr)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)
  \ge \bigl(\tfrac12\ln\lambda\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $a$ is constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integral identity is $\tfrac12\log q(1)-\tfrac12\log q(0)$. Expanding
$\int a^2$ as $(\int a)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)$ gives the bound.
\end{proof}

Thus $\cR$ measures only the temporal variance of the log-covariance rate.
Its unconstrained Class~Q minimizer is $a\equiv\tfrac12\ln\lambda$, i.e.\
$q(t)=\lambda^t$, which is \citet[Example~3.3]{lipschitzguided2025} in one
dimension. In several dimensions the per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$
attains $\cR=\bigl(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|\bigr)^2$. If $\Sigma_1$ has
at least two distinct eigenvalues, that path is not a Class~S shared
interpolant: matching $\alpha^2+\lambda_i\sigma^2=\lambda_i^t$ on two
distinct eigenvalues forces $\sigma(t)\asymp\sqrt{t}$ as $t\to 0$. If
three or more distinct eigenvalues are present, no shared
$(\alpha,\sigma)$ exists even in Class~R, because
$\alpha^2+\lambda\sigma^2$ is affine in $\lambda$ while $\lambda^t$ is
not. Isotropic spectra remain compatible with a shared schedule. We do
not claim that $d\ge 2$ forbids a shared schedule.

\section{Certified scalar Gaussian ranking inversion}
\label{sec:scalar}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:scalar}
The endpoints $X_0\sim N(0,1)$ and $X_1\sim N(0,4)$ are independent.
The compared schedules are linear and trigonometric VP, both in Class~S.
The solver is explicit Heun with NFE $B=8$, hence $S=4$ steps of size
$h=1/4$. Error is Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint laws, which equals
$|r-2|$ because every Heun factor below is positive.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:scalar}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:scalar},
\begin{equation}
  \cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{5\pi}{8}-1,\qquad
  \cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\frac{\pi^2}{16},
\end{equation}
and the comparison $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}>\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}$ is equivalent to
$2<\pi<8$. Averaged regularity therefore prefers VP. The Heun endpoint
factors satisfy
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{6797469}{3559400},\qquad
  0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}<\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
the second bound being a rational enclosure obtained by substituting
$\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ into the exact element of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{321331}{3559400}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}},
\end{equation}
so equal-NFE Gaussian $\wtwo$ prefers the linear path. Pairwise ordering
by integrated Jacobian regularity therefore need not agree with fixed-NFE
Heun $\wtwo$ error.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear drift is $a(t)=(5t-1)/(1-2t+5t^2)$. Completing the square and
using $\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2)=\pi/2$ yields $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$.
The VP integral evaluates to $\pi^2/16$. Multiplying
$5\pi/8-1>\pi^2/16$ by $16$ rearranges to $\pi^2-10\pi+16<0$, i.e.\
$(\pi-2)(\pi-8)<0$, which holds because $2<\pi<8$. The four linear Heun
factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, whose
product is $6797469/3559400$. Then $321331/3559400<91/1000$ is an
elementary comparison of integers. The VP grid drifts are exact elements
of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; the four-step Heun product has only
nonnegative coefficients, so substituting the rational upper bounds is
valid and gives $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$. Details occupy
Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}.
\end{proof}

One certified inversion proves the logical non-implication. It does not
by itself identify a unique causal mechanism, and it does not refute a
universal pairwise theorem that Chen et al.\ did not state.

\section{Solver-specific local error}
\label{sec:local}

The leading exact-minus-method coefficients of the one-step factor on
$x'=a(t)x$ are $\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for explicit Euler and
$\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3$ for the implemented Heun factor
$1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t)+a(t+h)(1+ha(t))\bigr)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:solvers}).
These involve different derivatives, signs, and stage locations.
The scalar integral of $\|A\|_2^2$ does not determine these derivatives,
signs, or stagewise values. The expansions establish that
local solver error depends on temporal and stagewise information not
determined by integrated Jacobian energy. They do not isolate sign loss
as the unique source of the certified inversion.

The telescoping identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n=0}^{S-1} r_{i,n}-\prod_{n=0}^{S-1} e_{i,n}
  =\sum_{n=0}^{S-1}(r_{i,n}-e_{i,n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
converts those local coefficients into endpoint factor error.
The exact modal factor is
$e_{i,n}=\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$; $r_{i,n}$ is numerical.
For any independent linear coupling,
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(0)=-1<0<a_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)=+1$. The VP drift vanishes
at the endpoints and does not change sign on $(0,1)$. This distinction
is consistent with the Heun inversion; it is not a causal isolation.

\section{Grid-aware impossibility}
\label{sec:gridaware}

Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} compares two fixed Class~S paths. The
following theorem addresses a different question: even the global
minimizer of integrated squared field energy need not minimize
fixed-grid Euler error. The competitor is chosen after the grid.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:gridaware}
Fix $L>0$ and an integer $N\ge 1$. Let $h=1/N$ and let forward Euler use
the left-endpoint grid $t_n=nh$ on $[0,1]$. Set
$\varepsilon_N=N(e^{L/N}-1)-L>0$ and define $a_0(t)\equiv L$ and
$a_{1,N}(t)=L+\varepsilon_N\cos(2\pi Nt)$. Consider the scalar ODE
$x'=a(t)x$ with $x(0)=1$, and the Class~S Gaussian interpolants of
Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1} with endpoints $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,e^{2L})$. Then:
both exact endpoints equal $e^{L}$; $a_0$ uniquely minimizes
$\int_0^1 a^2$ among scalar fields with integral $L$;
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2>\int_0^1 a_0^2$; left-endpoint
Euler is exact for $a_{1,N}$; and Euler has strictly positive endpoint
error for $a_0$. Endpoint error may equivalently be read as
$|r-e^{L}|$ or as Gaussian $\wtwo$ between $N(0,r^2)$ and $N(0,e^{2L})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The solution of $x'=a(t)x$, $x(0)=1$, is
$x(t)=\exp\bigl(\int_0^t a(s)\,ds\bigr)$. The cosine perturbation has
integer period, so $\int_0^1\cos(2\pi Nt)\,dt=0$ and both exact endpoints
equal $e^{L}$. Expanding the square and using the same orthogonality gives
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2$. The inequality
$\varepsilon_N>0$ is $e^z>1+z$ at $z=L/N>0$. Uniqueness of the
minimizer is Proposition~\ref{prop:cs}. On the left-endpoint Euler grid,
$\cos(2\pi Nt_n)=\cos(2\pi n)=1$, so
$1+h a_{1,N}(t_n)=e^{L/N}$ and the product is $e^{L}$. For the constant
field the product is $(1+L/N)^N<e^{L}$ because $\log(1+z)<z$ for
$z=L/N>0$. The Class~S embedding is Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1}.
\end{proof}

The field $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid. Shifted nodes, phase
offsets, and frequencies other than $N$ destroy exact Euler aliasing
(Table~\ref{tab:gridaware} in Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). Heun is not
exact on the aligned oscillation. The construction is a
solver-grid-dependent impossibility result, not evidence that naturally
occurring schedules typically behave this way.

\section{Finite Gaussian enumeration}
\label{sec:experiments}

Source $N(0,I)$. Four centered targets, with seed $271828$ for the
headline factor matrices: anisotropic Gaussians with condition number
$4$ in dimensions $2$ and $8$ (eigenvalues a geometric sequence from
$1/2$ to $2$); and factor-plus-noise covariances
$\Sigma=FF^\top+0.05 I$ with $F\in\RR^{d\times 2}$ having i.i.d.\
standard-normal entries. When $d=2$ the factor has rank equal to the
ambient dimension; when $d=8$ it is rank $2$ plus a noise floor. The
dimension-$2$ eigenvalues are $\numLrTwoMin$ and $\numLrTwoMax$; the
dimension-$8$ eigenvalues are $0.05$ (six times), $\numLrEightA$, and
$\numLrEightB$. Paths, solvers, budgets, and status occupy
Table~\ref{tab:setup}. Precision is float64, audited against an
$80$-digit Gelbrich reference. Phase~4 is confirmatory on a grid frozen
before the rerun, not a protocol recorded before discovery.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and stochastic interpolants supply the Gaussian path
family~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Lipschitz-guided schedule design proposes the selection criterion tested
here~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}: minimize $A_2$ over scalar schedules,
with a transfer formula that applies a designed schedule at inference
without retraining. The present negative result is a limitation of a
universal pairwise surrogate reading, not a refutation of that
optimization programme.

\citet{tao2026strain} give a broader dynamical error analysis, decomposing
$\nabla v=S+\Omega$ and controlling Euler error by the logarithmic norm
$\mu_2=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, together with material-derivative structure and
learned-model experiments. In one dimension $\mu_2(a)=a$ and
$\|A\|_2=|a|$. Their Theorem~5 concerns McCann displacement interpolation,
which is not the independent-coupling linear or VP path studied here.
The present contribution is narrower: an exact, fully certified scalar
Gaussian ranking inversion, a closed-form $W_2$ comparison, a
solver-grid-aware impossibility construction, and a transparent finite
Gaussian enumeration. It does not provide a general numerical-error
theory.

\citet{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} prove sharp Lipschitz regularity of
canonical flow-matching fields and Euler-type $\wtwo$ rates of order
$\sqrt{d}/N$ up to logs. That is a bound, not a pairwise ranking of two
interpolants at equal NFE. Solver-specific schedules and exact linear
parts are documented in diffusion
practice~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm,lu2022dpmsolver,zhang2023deis}.
\citet{benton2024error} bound $\wtwo$ of a learned flow. Those objects
are distinct from the ranking question tested here.

Complementary solver-aware approaches use temporal information rather
than an integrated spatial-Jacobian energy.
\citet{gupta2026sharpen} construct SharpEuler, calibrating Euler grids
from trajectory acceleration estimated on pretrained flow-matching
models. \citet{khan2026isofm} regularize material acceleration during
training. Other work modifies the coupling or prior design to produce
straighter, OT-like flows~\citep{malnick2026otdesign}. The present
contribution is neither a sampler nor a training regularizer: it gives
a certified ranking non-implication and a solver-grid-aware
impossibility result for the stated classes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The systems are commuting affine Gaussian interpolants. No learned neural
field, image, video, or world-model quantity is reported. The solvers are
three fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods at small equal-NFE budgets.
Exponential integrators solve affine scalar modes exactly, so the
comparisons concern generic RK discretizations of those modes rather than
the best integrator for an affine field. Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware}
depends on the grid: the competitor is chosen after the grid and exploits
exact aliasing. Census counts are hierarchical enumerations of specified
candidates, not population frequencies. No counterexample to the signed
logarithmic norm $\mu_2$ is provided. Sign loss is consistent with the
Heun inversion and is not isolated as its unique cause.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An integrated unsigned Jacobian-energy functional does not contain enough
solver-specific temporal information to universally determine few-step
endpoint error. A certified scalar Gaussian example shows that pairwise
ordering by integrated Jacobian regularity can disagree with fixed-NFE
Heun $\wtwo$ error. Local solver error depends on temporal and stagewise
information not determined by that energy. A solver-grid-aware Gaussian
construction shows that the global integrated-regularity minimizer need
not minimize fixed-grid Euler error. A specified finite Gaussian
enumeration contains both agreement and disagreement. The results
establish a limitation of a universal surrogate interpretation.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}}
\label{app:scalar}

\paragraph{Linear drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(1-t)^2+4t^2=1-2t+5t^2$ and
$q'= -2+10t$, so
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(5t-1)/q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)$. Completing the square yields the two equivalent identities
\begin{align}
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\Bigl[(t-\tfrac15)^2+\bigl(\tfrac25\bigr)^2\Bigr],
  \label{eq:q-bracket}\\
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\bigl(t-\tfrac15\bigr)^2+\tfrac45.
  \label{eq:q-plus}
\end{align}
Thus
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(t-1/5)/\bigl((t-1/5)^2+(2/5)^2\bigr)$.
The substitution $u=t-1/5=(2/5)\tan\theta$ gives
$du=(2/5)\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and converts $\int a_{\mathrm{lin}}^2$ into
$(5/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. The definite limits are
$\theta(0)=\arctan(-1/2)$ and $\theta(1)=\arctan 2$. The antiderivative
$(5/4)(\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta)$ therefore contributes
$(5/4)(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))-(5/4)(4/5)$. Both angles lie in
$(0,\pi/2)$ and $\tan(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))$ is undefined, so the sum is
$\pi/2$. Hence $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$. (The subtracted $1$ is the
boundary evaluation of $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\tan\theta/(1+\tan^2\theta)$.)

\paragraph{VP drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\cos^2(\pi t/2)+4\sin^2(\pi t/2)=(5-3\cos(\pi t))/2$ and
$q'=(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\frac{(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)}{5-3\cos(\pi t)}
  =\frac{(3\pi/4)\sin(\pi t)}{q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)}.
\end{equation}
The change of variables $u=\pi t$ yields
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=(9\pi/4)\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du$. The
standard Weierstrass evaluation of the integrand gives
$\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du=\pi/36$, hence
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\pi^2/16$. The comparison
$\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}>\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}$ rearranges to
$\pi^2-10\pi+16<0$, i.e.\ $(\pi-2)(\pi-8)<0$, which holds because
$2<\pi<8$. Both closed forms were checked by adaptive
quadrature (absolute discrepancy below $10^{-12}$; quadrature error
estimates are not used as certificates).

\paragraph{Heun recurrence.}
On $x'=a(t)x$ the implemented Heun factor is
$r=1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t_n)+a(t_{n+1})(1+h a(t_n))\bigr)$. With $h=1/4$ the four
linear factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, all
positive, with product $6797469/3559400$. Then $2-r=321331/3559400$, and
$321331\cdot 1000<91\cdot 3559400$ is an integer comparison, so
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091$.

\paragraph{Exact VP grid and rational certificate.}
At the Heun nodes the VP drift takes the values
\begin{equation}
  \begin{array}{c@{\quad}ccccc}
    t & 0 & 1/4 & 1/2 & 3/4 & 1 \\
    a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t) &
      0 &
      \tfrac{(9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82} &
      \tfrac{3\pi}{10} &
      \tfrac{(-9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82} &
      0
  \end{array}
\end{equation}
Positivity of $a(3/4)$ follows from $15\sqrt{2}>9$, or $15^2\cdot 2-9^2=369>0$
after squaring. Each Heun factor is therefore strictly positive, so
$0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}=2-r_{\mathrm{VP}}$.
The four-step product is the following element $P(\pi,\sqrt{2})$ of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$, obtained by expanding the four Heun factors
and reducing $\sqrt{2}^2=2$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P(\pi,\sqrt{2})
&= 1+\frac{3}{40}\pi+\frac{15}{164}\pi\sqrt{2}
+\frac{78579}{10758400}\pi^2+\frac{45}{5248}\pi^2\sqrt{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{11421}{17213440}\pi^3
+\frac{4671}{17213440}\pi^3\sqrt{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{5103}{275415040}\pi^4
+\frac{567}{55083008}\pi^4\sqrt{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{243}{2203320320}\pi^5
+\frac{729}{2203320320}\pi^5\sqrt{2}
+\frac{729}{176265625600}\pi^6.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Each of the twelve coefficients is a ratio of nonnegative integers, hence
nonnegative, and the constant term is $1$. Substituting any pair of
upper bounds for $\pi$ and $\sqrt{2}$ is therefore valid.
The bound $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ is the integer comparison $99^2-2\cdot 70^2=1$.
The bound $\pi<355/113$ follows from Machin's formula
$\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$ together with Leibniz remainders: seven
Taylor terms upper-bound $\arctan(1/5)$ and four terms lower-bound
$\arctan(1/239)$. The resulting rational is strictly less than $355/113$ by
an integer comparison. Substituting these enclosures yields
\begin{equation}
  P\Bigl(\frac{355}{113},\frac{99}{70}\Bigr)
  =\frac{\vpHeunBoundNum}{\vpHeunBoundDen}
  <\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
equivalently the integer comparison
\begin{equation}
  \vpHeunIntLeft<\vpHeunIntRight.
\end{equation}
Hence $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
An independent interval enclosure at $40$ decimal digits and an $80$-digit
non-interval product agree with this bound and with the float64
implementation to $10^{-14}$. They are cross-checks, not the proof.

\section{Solver formulas and local truncation expansions}
\label{app:solvers}

On $[t,t+h]$ for $x'=a(t)x$,
\begin{align}
  r_{\mathrm{Euler}}&=1+ha(t),\\
  r_{\mathrm{Heun}}&=1+\tfrac h2\bigl(a(t)+a(t+h)(1+ha(t))\bigr),
\end{align}
and classical RK4 with stages at $t$, $t+h/2$ (twice), and $t+h$. Equal NFE
requires $B\equiv 0\pmod s$ and sets $S=B/s$.

Assume $a$ is $C^3$. The exact factor admits
$1+ha+(h^2/2)(a'+a^2)+(h^3/6)(a''+3aa'+a^3)+O(h^4)$. Subtracting Euler
leaves $\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2+O(h^3)$. Substituting $a(t+h)$ into the
implemented Heun factor leaves $\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3+O(h^4)$. The
$aa'$ terms cancel. The expansion was confirmed with independent symbolic
algebra on the implemented expression.

\section{Class~S embedding of the grid-aware fields}
\label{app:p4p1}

The ODE comparison of Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware} is proved in the main
text. This appendix records the Class~S Gaussian embedding and the
positivity hypotheses. The field $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid. The
construction is not the mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}.

The same fields are Class~S Gaussian interpolants. Set $\lambda=e^{2L}$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  q_k(t)&=\exp\Bigl(2\int_0^t a_k(s)\,ds\Bigr),\\
  \alpha_k(t)&=\sqrt{q_k(t)}\,\cos(\pi t/2),\\
  \sigma_k(t)&=\sqrt{q_k(t)/\lambda}\,\sin(\pi t/2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then $q_k(0)=1$, $\int_0^1 a_k=L$ so $q_k(1)=\lambda$, and the endpoint
conditions $\alpha_k(0)=\sigma_k(1)=1$, $\alpha_k(1)=\sigma_k(0)=0$ hold.
Positivity $q_k>0$ and $C^\infty$ regularity of $a_k$ give
$\alpha_k,\sigma_k\in C^\infty([0,1])$, hence the embedding belongs to
Class~S. The identity
$\alpha_k^2+\lambda\sigma_k^2=q_k$ is trigonometric, and
$q_k'/(2q_k)=a_k$ by construction. Among scalar fields with fixed integral
$L$, $a_0$ uniquely minimizes $\int_0^1 a^2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cs}).
Euler has strictly positive endpoint error for $a_0$, while $a_{1,N}$ has
strictly larger regularity and left-endpoint Euler is exact for $a_{1,N}$.
Thus $a_0$ is an admissible Class~S $\cR$-minimizer among one-dimensional
variance paths from $1$ to $\lambda$,
yet left-endpoint Euler has strictly larger endpoint error for $a_0$ than
for the higher-$\cR$ path $a_{1,N}$. This construction blocks any inference
from a $36$ of $36$ count among four specified candidate paths on $36$
tested blocks to the statement that a global $\cR$-minimizer minimizes
fixed-NFE error.

\section{Non-centered structural stress test}
\label{app:noncentered}

The quantity $\cR$ depends only on $A(t)$. If $c(t)\ne 0$, the mean still
contributes $\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ to $\wtwo^2$. Dimensions $d\in\{2,8\}$,
coordinates indexed by $i=0,\ldots,d-1$. Source $N(\mu_0,I)$ with
$\mu_{0,i}=0.75(-1)^i$. Target $N(\mu_1,D)$ with $\mu_{1,i}=1+0.25\,i$ and
$D=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$, where the $\lambda_i$ form a geometric
sequence from $6^{-1/2}$ to $6^{1/2}$ (condition number $6$). Then
$c(t)\ne 0$. Paths are linear and trigonometric VP. Solvers are Euler, Heun,
and RK4 at NFE $B\in\{8,16,32\}$. Write $M=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ and
$C=\wtwo^2-M$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}=M_{\mathrm{lin}}-M_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}=C_{\mathrm{lin}}-C_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{total}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\end{equation}
A block is mean-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$
and $|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|$, and
covariance-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$ and
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|$. Otherwise it is mixed.
The same paths, solvers, and budgets give \nWorkshopBlocks{} blocks, of
which \nWorkshopInversions{} invert under continuous $\cR$.
Table~\ref{tab:workshop} reports the driver of each path preference.
Several inversions, including all dimension-$8$ Euler and Heun inversions,
are mean-driven: $\cR$ cannot see them. The family was specified before
execution as a stress test of this structural blind spot, not as an
independent replication of the centered inversions.

\section{Quadrature, precision, and interval procedures}

Frozen Gaussian $\wtwo$ on the centered census was checked against an
$80$-digit Gelbrich reference. The maximum observed gap is the numerical
floor $\numPrecFloor$. The paired concordance score
$(n_{\mathrm{agree}}-n_{\mathrm{invert}})/n$ between the $\cR$ order and the
$\wtwo$ order is a descriptive census statistic, not a test
(Table~\ref{tab:concordance}). On the $36$ linear-versus-VP blocks,
the score is $2/9$. Stratified by solver ($n=12$ blocks per stratum) the
score is $+1/2$ under Euler and RK4 and $-1/3$ under Heun. The two
anisotropic geometries share one $\cR$ pair, a structural tie in the
value of $\cR$, not in the denominator. Stratified by geometry
($n=9$ blocks per stratum) the scores are $1/3$, $5/9$, $-1/3$, and
$1/3$.

Scalar linear Heun factors are computed in exact rationals. The VP Heun
product is certified by substituting $\pi<355/113$ and $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ into
a nonnegative element of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; the ranking inequality
is the integer comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}. An independent
interval enclosure at $40$ decimal digits
(\texttt{mpmath}~\mpmathVersion) and an $80$-digit (non-interval) product
are recorded as cross-checks against the float64 Heun implementation.
Default numerical precision for the benchmark runs is float64.

Table~\ref{tab:lebesgue} reports the log-Lebesgue measure of the
linear-versus-VP inversion set on $[0.05,100]$. The declared resolution is
\logLebesgueNodes{} log-uniform nodes; doubling to $2561$ nodes changes
every cell by less than $1\%$.

\section{Robustness grids}
\label{app:robust}

Table~\ref{tab:gridaware} records aligned and perturbed evaluations of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gridaware} at $L=\log 2$. Ranking inversion is recorded
only when exact endpoints still match and the constant minimizer has
strictly larger solver error. Phase offsets and interior nodes preserve
endpoints but destroy Euler exactness. Non-integer frequencies break the
common-endpoint constraint. Heun is not exact on the aligned oscillation.
These checks show how quickly the aliasing phenomenon disappears when
grid alignment is perturbed; they are not a population frequency.
