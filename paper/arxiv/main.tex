\begin{abstract}
Few-step sampling of a continuous-time generative model requires a probability
path, a numerical solver, and a budget of field evaluations (NFE).
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing averaged squared Jacobian
regularity $A_2$ as a path-selection criterion. This paper asks whether the
order that this scalar induces between two interpolants is reliable at fixed
NFE. The implication fails already for centered Gaussian interpolants.

The main logical result is a one-dimensional, seed-free counterexample.
For $X_0\sim N(0,1)$ and $X_1\sim N(0,4)$ independent, the exact regularity
integrals of the linear and trigonometric variance-preserving (VP) paths are
$5\pi/8-1$ and $\pi^2/16$. Explicit Heun with NFE $8$ (four steps of size
$1/4$) reverses the ranking of Gaussian $\wtwo$ endpoint errors. The linear
Heun factor is the rational $6797469/3559400$. The VP factor lies in
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; substituting the documented bounds
$\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ yields a purely rational certificate of
$r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$, hence
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}$.

A commuting modal identity then accounts for what the unsigned average
discards: solver-stage locations, signs, cancellation, and transport of local
factor defects. On four centered geometries the per-mode log-covariance
schedule (Chen et al.\ Example~3.3), which minimizes $\cR$, attains the
smallest $\wtwo$ in all \nBlocks{} solver-budget blocks. Linear versus VP
invert in \nInversionCells{} of \nGeometrySolverCells{}
geometry$\times$solver cells (\nAllNFEInvertCells{} of
\nGeometrySolverCells{} at every NFE in $\{8,16,32\}$), from
\nDistinctRComparisons{} distinct $\cR$ comparisons. Thus $\cR$ is reliable
as an objective on this family and not reliable as a linear-versus-VP
comparator away from that optimum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A continuous-time generative sampler is specified by four distinct objects:
a probability path (an interpolant or noise schedule), the associated
marginal velocity field, a numerical ODE solver, and an evaluation budget.
Classical one-step bounds involve Lipschitz constants of the
field~\citep{hairer1993solving}. In the stochastic interpolant framework,
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} propose minimizing averaged squared drift
Lipschitzness (their Definition~3.2, denoted $A_2$) as a
\emph{selection} criterion. They prove no bound relating $A_2$ to a
discretization error. We test whether the pairwise order that $A_2$ induces
between two admissible paths is reliable at equal NFE.

This paper isolates that ranking question on commuting Gaussian interpolants,
where the marginal field is affine, Gaussian $\wtwo$ is closed-form, and the
regularity integrand is known exactly. The leading result is not a large
grid. It is a deterministic scalar example, proved by an exact integral, an
exact rational Heun product, and a rational enclosure of the VP product
(Section~\ref{sec:scalar}). The subsequent modal analysis explains which
information $\cR$ discards (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). The Cauchy--Schwarz
minimizer of $\cR$ and a $\lambda$-sweep then separate $\cR$ as an objective
from $\cR$ as a linear-versus-VP comparator
(Section~\ref{sec:third}). A hierarchical
benchmark and a non-centered stress test show that linear-versus-VP inversions
are not confined to the scalar pair
(Sections~\ref{sec:benchmark}--\ref{sec:noncentered}).

Contemporaneous work studies Lipschitz bounds for Euler
rates~\citep{stephanovitch2026lipschitz}, sharpness-aware
grids~\citep{gupta2026sharpen}, least-action paths~\citep{du2026lagrangian},
and instance-aware discretizations~\citep{yuan2026instance}. Those results
control other objects. \citet{tao2026strain} give the general
strain/vorticity form of the phenomenon; Section~\ref{sec:related} records
the precise relation.

\section{Problem formulation and notation}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Interpolant.}
Let $X_0\sim N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$ and $X_1\sim N(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)$ be independent
in $\RR^d$, with $\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1$ symmetric positive definite. Let
$\alpha,\sigma:[0,1]\to\RR$ be $C^3$ and satisfy the boundary conditions
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(0)=1,\quad \sigma(0)=0,\quad \alpha(1)=0,\quad \sigma(1)=1.
\end{equation}
The interpolant is
\begin{equation}
  X_t=\alpha(t)X_0+\sigma(t)X_1,
  \label{eq:interpolant}
\end{equation}
as in stochastic interpolants and flow matching~\citep{albergo2025stochastic,lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Throughout, $\alpha^2+\sigma^2$ is not required to equal $1$ except on the VP
path. The two paths compared below are the linear schedule
$(\alpha,\sigma)=(1-t,\,t)$ and the trigonometric VP schedule
$(\cos(\pi t/2),\,\sin(\pi t/2))$.

The law of $X_t$ is Gaussian with mean $m_t=\alpha\mu_0+\sigma\mu_1$ and
covariance $Q(t)=\alpha^2\Sigma_0+\sigma^2\Sigma_1$. We assume $Q(t)\succ 0$
on $[0,1]$. The marginal velocity of~\eqref{eq:interpolant} is the affine
field
\begin{equation}
  b(t,x)=A(t)\,x+c(t),\qquad
  A(t)=C_t Q(t)^{-1},\qquad
  c(t)=\dot m_t-A(t)m_t,
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
with $C_t=\dot\alpha\alpha\,\Sigma_0+\dot\sigma\sigma\,\Sigma_1$. This is a
marginal interpolant ODE, not a score-based probability-flow ODE in the sense
of~\citet{song2021score}, although the latter is an instance of a generative
ODE. If $\mu_0=\mu_1=0$ and $\Sigma_0=I$, then $c\equiv 0$ and
$A=\tfrac12 Q'Q^{-1}$.

\paragraph{Isotropic source.}
If $\Sigma_0=I$, then $Q(t)$, $Q'(t)$, $A(t)$, and $\Sigma_1$ share the
eigenbasis of $\Sigma_1$. Simultaneous diagonalization with the identity is
not an extra hypothesis. In that basis, mode $i$ with eigenvalue
$\lambda_i>0$ obeys $x_i'=a_i(t)x_i+c_i(t)$ with
$q_i(t)=\alpha(t)^2+\lambda_i\sigma(t)^2$ and
$a_i(t)=q_i'(t)/(2q_i(t))$. The covariance drift formula depends only on the
variance schedule. It is independent of the mean path $c(t)=\dot m_t-A(t)m_t$.
The exact one-step factor on
$[t_n,t_{n+1}]$ is $\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$. For centered modes the
exact endpoint factor is $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

\paragraph{Regularity.}
Following \citet[Definition~3.2]{lipschitzguided2025},
\begin{equation}
  \cR[b]=\int_0^1 \bigl\|A(t)\bigr\|_2^2\,dt.
  \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
For state-independent $A(t)$ this coincides with their path expectation.
By definition $\cR$ ignores $c(t)$. For commuting $A(t)$,
$\|A(t)\|_2=\max_i |a_i(t)|$. Headline comparisons in this paper use the
continuous integral~\eqref{eq:R}. The historical 24-node trapezoidal
estimator is written $\Rhat$ and is used only as an implementation check.
No trapezoidal value is described as an exact integral.

Any admissible one-dimensional variance path from $1$ to $\lambda>0$
obeys $\int_0^1 a\,dt=\tfrac12\ln\lambda$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \cR=\int_0^1 a^2\,dt=\Bigl(\int_0^1 a\,dt\Bigr)^2+\mathrm{Var}_t(a)
  \ge \bigl(\tfrac12\ln\lambda\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if $a$ is constant. The minimizer is
$q(t)=\lambda^t$, $a\equiv\tfrac12\ln\lambda$, which is
\citet[Example~3.3]{lipschitzguided2025}. In several dimensions the
per-mode schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$ yields
$\cR=\bigl(\tfrac12\max_i|\ln\lambda_i|\bigr)^2$. That schedule is not in
general a shared $(\alpha,\sigma)$ interpolant.

\paragraph{Solver, step, and NFE.}
Euler uses $s=1$ field evaluation per step, Heun $s=2$, and classical RK4
$s=4$. A budget $B$ is admitted only if $B\equiv 0\pmod s$. The step count
is $S=B/s$ and the grid is the uniform partition of $[0,1]$ with $S$ steps.
Equal NFE is not equal step count: at $B=8$, Euler takes $8$ steps, Heun $4$,
and RK4 $2$.

\paragraph{Endpoint error.}
A solver applied to~\eqref{eq:affine} produces an affine endpoint map
$x\mapsto Rx+d$. Source moments are pushed through this map. Error is the
Gaussian $\wtwo$ of the endpoint \emph{laws}~\citep{gelbrich1990formula,peyre2019computational},
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^2=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2
  +\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\hat C+\Sigma_1-2(\Sigma_1^{1/2}\hat C\Sigma_1^{1/2})^{1/2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is neither pathwise trajectory error nor local truncation error. In the
centered commuting case, $\wtwo^2=\sum_i\bigl(|r_i|-\sqrt{\lambda_i}\bigr)^2$.

\paragraph{Question.}
Does $\cR[b]<\cR[b']$ imply $E[b]<E[b']$ at equal NFE? The answer is no,
already in dimension one.

\section{A one-dimensional Gaussian ranking counterexample}
\label{sec:scalar}

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:scalar}
The endpoints $X_0\sim N(0,1)$ and $X_1\sim N(0,4)$ are independent.
The compared schedules are linear and trigonometric VP. The solver is
explicit Heun with NFE $B=8$, hence $S=4$ steps of size $h=1/4$.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:scalar}, $q(t)=\alpha(t)^2+4\sigma(t)^2$ and
$a(t)=q'(t)/(2q(t))$. The exact endpoint factor is $2$. All Heun factors
below are positive, so $\wtwo=\bigl||r|-2\bigr|=|r-2|$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:scalar}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:scalar},
\begin{align}
  \cR_{\mathrm{lin}}&=\frac{5\pi}{8}-1
  \approx 0.9634954084936207,\\
  \cR_{\mathrm{VP}}&=\frac{\pi^2}{16}
  \approx 0.6168502750680849,
\end{align}
so averaged regularity prefers VP. The Heun endpoint factors satisfy
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{6797469}{3559400},\qquad
  0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}<\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
the second bound being a rational enclosure obtained by substituting
$\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ into the exact element of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}=\frac{321331}{3559400}<0.091<0.130<\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}},
\end{equation}
so equal-NFE Gaussian $\wtwo$ prefers the linear path. The regularity
ordering does not determine the fixed-NFE $\wtwo$ ordering.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The linear drift is $a(t)=(5t-1)/(1-2t+5t^2)$. Completing the square to
$5(t-1/5)^2+4/5$ and using $\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2)=\pi/2$ yields
$\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$.
The VP integral evaluates to $\pi^2/16$. The four linear Heun factors are
the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, whose product is
$6797469/3559400$. Then
$321331/3559400<91/1000$ is an elementary comparison of integers.
The VP grid drifts are exact elements of $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$; the
four-step Heun product has only nonnegative coefficients, so substituting
the rational upper bounds $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ and $\pi<355/113$ is valid and
gives $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$, hence $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
Details occupy Appendix~\ref{app:scalar}.
\end{proof}

  $\wtwo$ at NFE $8$ (right). The VP error bar is the certified lower bound

One certified inversion proves the logical non-implication. The remainder of
the paper shows that the phenomenon is not an artefact of this scalar pair,
and identifies the information that $\cR$ cannot retain.

\section{Modal analysis and transported defects}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Let $e_{i,n}=\sqrt{q_i(t_{n+1})/q_i(t_n)}$ be the exact factor of mode $i$
and let $r_{i,n}$ be the numerical one-step factor
(Appendix~\ref{app:solvers}). The algebraic identity
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{n=0}^{S-1} r_{i,n}-\prod_{n=0}^{S-1} e_{i,n}
  =\sum_{n=0}^{S-1}(r_{i,n}-e_{i,n})
  \Bigl(\prod_{j<n}r_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \Bigl(\prod_{j>n}e_{i,j}\Bigr)
  \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
holds without a non-vanishing hypothesis: it is the telescoping expansion of
a product. Prefixes are numerical; suffixes are exact. Endpoint factor error
is therefore a transported sum of signed, solver-stage-dependent local
defects.

Identity
\eqref{eq:telescope} is an accounting tool. The content of the inversion is
the leading exact-minus-method coefficient:
$\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2$ for Euler and
$\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3$ for the implemented Heun factor
$1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t)+a(t+h)(1+ha(t))\bigr)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:lte}). They
involve different derivatives, signs, and stage locations. Averaging
$\|A\|_2^2$ discards stage times, signs, cancellation, and
transport.

For any independent linear coupling,
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(0)=-1<0<a_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)=+1$. The VP drift vanishes at
the endpoints and does not change sign on $(0,1)$. The unsigned average
therefore erases a qualitative distinction that every fixed-stage
Runge--Kutta method samples.

Figure~\ref{fig:eigenmode} shows the signed transported terms on
the strongest centered inversion (low-rank, $d=8$, Euler, NFE $8$). The
identity is exact for that block; the figure is an illustration, not an
independent causal test.

A complementary scalar construction (Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1}) shows
non-implication for left-endpoint Euler on a grid-aware oscillatory field.
The field $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid. That construction is not the mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} or of
the Gaussian benchmark.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The $\cR$-minimizer and the inversion region}{The R-minimizer and the inversion region}}
\label{sec:third}

The Cauchy--Schwarz argument of Section~\ref{sec:background} identifies a
third path: the per-mode log-covariance schedule $q_i(t)=\lambda_i^t$.
On the four centered geometries of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} it has the
smallest continuous $\cR$ in every block and the smallest Gaussian $\wtwo$
in all \nBlocks{} solver-budget conditions
(Appendix~\ref{app:logcov}). Linear versus VP remain free to invert.

A one-dimensional sweep $\lambda\in[0.05,100]$ locates those inversions
(Figure~\ref{fig:region}). At $\lambda=4$, the inversion is present under
Heun at every tested budget
NFE$\in\{4,8,12,16,24,32,64,128,256\}$, absent under Euler, and mixed
under RK4 (absent at NFE $8$ and $12$). Euler \emph{does} invert at more
extreme $\lambda$ on the same sweep. The scalar example is therefore
solver-dependent, not an artefact of Heun at budget $8$ alone.

\label{sec:benchmark}

Source $N(0,I)$.

Gaussians with condition number $4$ in dimensions $2$ and $8$

\section{Non-centered structural stress test}
\label{sec:noncentered}

The quantity $\cR$ depends only on $A(t)$. If $c(t)\ne 0$, the mean still
contributes $\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ to $\wtwo^2$. The following family was
specified in the repository before execution (commit \texttt{f5e857c} at
2026-07-24 15:28, results \texttt{8b87c7f} at 16:04). Amendment A1
(\texttt{508101e}, 15:33) changed only an auxiliary endpoint sanity check
from a $\wtwo$ threshold, which float64 identical Gaussians cannot meet, to
a moment gap. The family itself was not changed. The accurate description
is a pre-specified stress test of a structural blind spot of $\cR$, not an
independent replication of the centered mechanism.

\paragraph{Construction.}
Dimensions $d\in\{2,8\}$, with coordinates indexed by $i=0,\ldots,d-1$.
Source $N(\mu_0,I)$ with $\mu_{0,i}=0.75(-1)^i$. Target $N(\mu_1,D)$ with
$\mu_{1,i}=1+0.25\,i$ and $D=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$, where the
$\lambda_i$ form a geometric sequence from $6^{-1/2}$ to $6^{1/2}$
(condition number $6$). Then $c(t)\ne 0$. Paths are linear and trigonometric
VP. Solvers are Euler, Heun, and RK4 at NFE $B\in\{8,16,32\}$, with
$S=B/s$ uniform steps of size $1/S$ integrated forward in $t$
($s=1,2,4$). Reported $\wtwo$ values and the mean/Bures split are
decomposed from the frozen run
\path{outputs/workshop_external_validation_2026-07-24-v1/}; continuous
$\cR$ is recomputed from the same eigenvalues.

\paragraph{Signed differences.}
Write $M=\|\hat m-\mu_1\|_2^2$ and $C=\wtwo^2-M$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}=M_{\mathrm{lin}}-M_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}=C_{\mathrm{lin}}-C_{\mathrm{VP}},\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{total}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\end{equation}
A block is mean-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$
and $|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|$, and
covariance-driven if $\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}>0$ and
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{cov}}|>|\Delta_{\mathrm{mean}}|$. Otherwise it is mixed.
The same paths, solvers, and budgets give \nWorkshopBlocks{} blocks, of
which \nWorkshopInversions{} invert under continuous $\cR$.
Table~\ref{tab:workshop} reports the driver of each path preference.
Several inversions, including all dimension-$8$ Euler and Heun inversions,
are mean-driven: $\cR$ cannot see them.

\section{Relation to existing work}
\label{sec:related}

Flow matching and stochastic interpolants supply the Gaussian path
family~\citep{lipman2023flow,albergo2025stochastic,liu2023rectified,tong2024conditional}.
Lipschitz-guided schedule design proposes the selection criterion tested
here~\citep{lipschitzguided2025}. Solver-specific schedules and exact linear
parts are documented in diffusion practice~\citep{sabour2024align,karras2022edm,lu2022dpmsolver,lu2022dpmsolverpp,zhang2023deis}.

\citet{stephanovitch2026lipschitz} prove sharp Lipschitz regularity of
canonical flow-matching fields and Euler-type $\wtwo$ rates of order
$\sqrt{d}/N$ up to logs. That is a bound, not a pairwise ranking of two
interpolants at equal NFE. \citet{gupta2026sharpen} allocate Euler steps
where a learned field is sharp; the path is held fixed. \citet{yuan2026instance}
learn instance-aware grids. \citet{du2026lagrangian} select paths by a
least-action principle. \citet{khan2026isofm} regularizes the material derivative
of learned fields.

\citet{tao2026strain} decompose $\nabla v=S+\Omega$ and control Euler error
by the logarithmic norm $\mu_2=\lambda_{\max}(S)$, which is signed, whereas
$\cR$ integrates $\|A\|_2^2$. In one dimension $\mu_2(a)=a$ and
$\|A\|_2=|a|$. Their Theorem~5 states that McCann displacement interpolation
has vanishing material derivative, hence Euler is of order $2$ in the
Eulerian analysis; Remark~7 records the stronger Lagrangian exactness
observed on that interpolant. Neither statement applies to the
independent-coupling linear or VP path studied here. The present paper
supplies a rationally certified Gaussian instance of ranking failure for
the unsigned average $\|A\|_2^2$. A counterexample to $\mu_2$ itself is
outside our scope.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The systems are commuting affine Gaussian interpolants, three
fixed-step Runge--Kutta methods, and small equal-NFE budgets. No learned
neural field, image, video, or world-model quantity is reported.
\citet{lipschitzguided2025} prove no discretization-error bound and do not
claim a universal pairwise ranking at fixed NFE; the example does not
invalidate $A_2$ as an optimization objective within a path family.
The Gaussian family is a probe in which $\cR$ and $\wtwo$ are closed form;
in a learned field the linear part is not exact, so exponential
integrators that solve an affine scalar mode exactly are not a drop-in
substitute. Cell counts and the \nInversions{} inverted rows are
hierarchical descriptive summaries, not population rates. The scalar
inversion at $\lambda=4$ is solver-dependent: present under Heun at every
tested budget, absent under Euler at that $\lambda$, and mixed under RK4.
The Euler construction of Appendix~\ref{app:p4p1} is grid-aware: the field
is chosen after the grid. Robustness NFE $64$ and $128$ and $\pm 10\%$
noise-floor perturbations are post-hoc. A pre-specified non-centered
family (Section~\ref{sec:noncentered}) shows a structural blind spot of
$\cR$ for the drift offset $c(t)$; it is not an independent replication of
the centered mechanism.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Averaged squared Jacobian regularity does not determine the equal-NFE
Gaussian $\wtwo$ ranking of the linear and VP interpolants. The fact is
proved by a one-dimensional Heun calculation whose integrals and linear
factor are exact and whose VP factor is enclosed by a rational substitution
in $\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. On the tested grid the Cauchy--Schwarz
minimizer of $\cR$ dominates in $\wtwo$, while linear versus VP invert in
\nInversionCells{} of \nGeometrySolverCells{} geometry$\times$solver cells.
Thus $\cR$ is reliable as an objective on this Gaussian family and not
reliable as a comparator of linear versus VP away from that optimum.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}}
\label{app:scalar}

\paragraph{Linear drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(1-t)^2+4t^2=1-2t+5t^2$ and
$q'= -2+10t$, so
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(5t-1)/q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)$. Completing the square yields the two equivalent identities
\begin{align}
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\Bigl[(t-\tfrac15)^2+\bigl(\tfrac25\bigr)^2\Bigr],
  \label{eq:q-bracket}\\
  q_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)
  &= 5\bigl(t-\tfrac15\bigr)^2+\tfrac45.
  \label{eq:q-plus}
\end{align}
Thus
$a_{\mathrm{lin}}(t)=(t-1/5)/\bigl((t-1/5)^2+(2/5)^2\bigr)$.
The substitution $u=t-1/5=(2/5)\tan\theta$ gives
$du=(2/5)\sec^2\theta\,d\theta$ and converts $\int a_{\mathrm{lin}}^2$ into
$(5/2)\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. The definite limits are
$\theta(0)=\arctan(-1/2)$ and $\theta(1)=\arctan 2$. The antiderivative
$(5/4)(\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta)$ therefore contributes
$(5/4)(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))-(5/4)(4/5)$. Both angles lie in
$(0,\pi/2)$ and $\tan(\arctan 2+\arctan(1/2))$ is undefined, so the sum is
$\pi/2$. Hence $\cR_{\mathrm{lin}}=5\pi/8-1$. (The subtracted $1$ is the
boundary evaluation of $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\tan\theta/(1+\tan^2\theta)$.)

\paragraph{VP drift and integral.}
$q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\cos^2(\pi t/2)+4\sin^2(\pi t/2)=(5-3\cos(\pi t))/2$ and
$q'=(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)$, hence
\begin{equation}
  a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=\frac{(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)}{5-3\cos(\pi t)}
  =\frac{(3\pi/4)\sin(\pi t)}{q_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)}.
\end{equation}
The change of variables $u=\pi t$ yields
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=(9\pi/4)\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du$. The
standard Weierstrass evaluation of the integrand gives
$\int_0^\pi \sin^2 u/(5-3\cos u)^2\,du=\pi/36$, hence
$\cR_{\mathrm{VP}}=\pi^2/16$. Both closed forms were checked by adaptive
quadrature (absolute discrepancy of the values below $10^{-12}$; the VP
error \emph{estimate} returned by \texttt{scipy.integrate.quad} can be larger
than that discrepancy and is not used as a certificate).

\paragraph{Heun recurrence.}
On $x'=a(t)x$ the implemented Heun factor is
$r=1+(h/2)\bigl(a(t_n)+a(t_{n+1})(1+h a(t_n))\bigr)$. With $h=1/4$ the four
linear factors are the rationals $47/52$, $6/5$, $497/370$, and $97/74$, all
positive, with product $6797469/3559400$. Then $2-r=321331/3559400$, and
$321331\cdot 1000<91\cdot 3559400$ is an integer comparison, so
$\wtwo^{\mathrm{lin}}<0.091$.

\paragraph{Exact VP grid and rational certificate.}
The VP drift is
$a_{\mathrm{VP}}(t)=(3\pi/2)\sin(\pi t)/(5-3\cos(\pi t))$. Evaluating at the
Heun nodes gives
\begin{align}
  a(0)&=0,\\
  a(1/4)&=\frac{(9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82},\\
  a(1/2)&=\frac{3\pi}{10},\\
  a(3/4)&=\frac{(-9+15\sqrt{2})\pi}{82},\\
  a(1)&=0.
\end{align}
Positivity of $a(3/4)$ follows from $15\sqrt{2}>9$, or $15^2\cdot 2-9^2=369>0$
after squaring. Each Heun factor is therefore strictly positive, so
$0<r_{\mathrm{VP}}$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}=2-r_{\mathrm{VP}}$.
The four-step product is an element $P(\pi,\sqrt{2})$ of
$\mathbb{Q}[\pi,\sqrt{2}]$. Expanding and reducing $\sqrt{2}^2=2$ produces
twelve monomials, all with nonnegative coefficients. Substituting any pair of
upper bounds for $\pi$ and $\sqrt{2}$ is therefore valid.
The bound $\sqrt{2}<99/70$ is the integer comparison $99^2-2\cdot 70^2=1$.
The bound $\pi<355/113$ follows from Machin's formula
$\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)$ together with Leibniz remainders: seven
Taylor terms upper-bound $\arctan(1/5)$ and four terms lower-bound
$\arctan(1/239)$. The resulting rational is strictly less than $355/113$ by
an integer comparison. Substituting these enclosures yields
\begin{equation}
  P\Bigl(\frac{355}{113},\frac{99}{70}\Bigr)
  =\frac{\vpHeunBoundNum}{\vpHeunBoundDen}
  <\frac{187}{100},
\end{equation}
equivalently the integer comparison
\begin{equation}
  \vpHeunIntLeft<\vpHeunIntRight.
\end{equation}
Hence $r_{\mathrm{VP}}<187/100$ and $\wtwo^{\mathrm{VP}}>13/100$.
Coefficient records occupy the compact artefact
\texttt{scalar\_counterexample.json}.
An independent \texttt{mpmath.iv} enclosure at $40$ decimal digits, using
\texttt{mpmath}~\mpmathVersion, and an $80$-digit non-interval product, agree
with this bound and with the package float64 implementation to $10^{-14}$.
They are cross-checks, not the proof.

\section{Local truncation expansions}
\label{app:lte}

Assume $a$ is $C^3$. The exact factor admits
$1+ha+(h^2/2)(a'+a^2)+(h^3/6)(a''+3aa'+a^3)+O(h^4)$. Subtracting Euler
leaves $\tfrac12(a'+a^2)h^2+O(h^3)$. Substituting $a(t+h)$ into the
implemented Heun factor leaves $\bigl(-a''/12+a^3/6\bigr)h^3+O(h^4)$. The
$aa'$ terms cancel. The expansion was confirmed with independent symbolic
algebra on the implemented expression.

\section{Grid-aware Euler construction}
\label{app:p4p1}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:euler}
Fix $L>0$ and an integer $N\ge 1$. Let $h=1/N$ and let forward Euler use the
left-endpoint grid $t_n=nh$ on $[0,1]$. Set
$\varepsilon_N=N(e^{L/N}-1)-L>0$ and define $a_0(t)=L$ and
$a_{1,N}(t)=L+\varepsilon_N\cos(2\pi Nt)$. For $x(0)=1$: both exact
endpoints equal $e^{L}$; $\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2>\int_0^1 a_0^2$;
Euler is exact for $a_{1,N}$; Euler has strictly positive endpoint error for
$a_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The solution of $x'=a(t)x$, $x(0)=1$, is
$x(t)=\exp\bigl(\int_0^t a(s)\,ds\bigr)$. The cosine perturbation has
integer period, so $\int_0^1\cos(2\pi Nt)\,dt=0$ and both exact endpoints
equal $e^{L}$. Expanding the square and using the same orthogonality gives
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2=L^2+\varepsilon_N^2/2$. The inequality
$\varepsilon_N>0$ is $e^z>1+z$ at $z=L/N>0$, hence
$\int_0^1 a_{1,N}^2>\int_0^1 a_0^2=L^2$.

On the left-endpoint Euler grid, $\cos(2\pi Nt_n)=\cos(2\pi n)=1$, so
$1+h a_{1,N}(t_n)=1+(L+\varepsilon_N)/N=e^{L/N}$. Every oscillatory Euler
factor equals $e^{L/N}$ and the product is $e^{L}$. For the constant field
every Euler factor equals $1+L/N$, and the product is $(1+L/N)^N$. Since
$\log(1+z)<z$ for $z=L/N>0$, one has $(1+L/N)^N<e^{L}$.
\end{proof}

The field $a_{1,N}$ is chosen after the grid. The construction is not the
mechanism of Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}. An 80-digit check confirms that
the oscillatory Euler product equals $e^{L}$, but it is not a substitute for
the argument above.
